# Mass Bridge — Problem Statement

Derive that solitonic particles acquire an effective rest mass from self-confinement of their wave mode in a closed loop, WITHOUT the Higgs mechanism.

## Physical picture

A circularly closed toroidal excitation is a wave trapped in an infinite self-confined loop. The wave cannot disperse because it is topologically trapped. Its total substrate energy E = m·c² defines the effective rest mass.

Key distinction from Higgs: mass here is not from spontaneous symmetry breaking and a scalar condensate, but from the energy of a topologically trapped, self-consistently confined wave. This is compatible with radiative corrections but not with the Higgs-origin-of-mass framework.

## Specific derivation tasks

**(a) Finite energy of toroidal mode.** For a toroidal mode ansatz (the electron-like sector), show that the total substrate energy E[Ψ_torus] is finite and bounded below, giving a lower bound on the effective mass. The ansatz: a U(1) line vortex (π₁ = ℤ) wrapped into a torus, with carrier rotation along the timelike worldtube axis giving the complex phase (Ψ ≈ ξ + (i/ω₀)ξ̇, i∂_tΨ = H_eff Ψ).

**(b) Equilibrium radius.** Show that the energy is a function of the loop radius R and the amplitude A, E = E(R, A), and identify the equilibrium radius where dE/dR = 0 as the physical particle size. The physical mechanism: W₂ ∝ R (spreading cost) and W₄ ∝ 1/R (confinement cost) balance at R* = √(E₄/E₂), the Derrick analog for the toroidal geometry.

**(c) Mass-frequency relation.** Show that the energy at equilibrium scales correctly with the carrier frequency ω₀ as E ≈ ℏω₀ in appropriate units. The U(1) carrier phase comes from the time direction (Ψ ≈ ξ + (i/ω₀)ξ̇); the energy stored in one carrier cycle is the substrate analog of the quantum of energy.
