## Counter-argument (model-specific)

The critique is correct that Bell alone does not force retrocausality. But the model has additional commitments:

1. Determinism (D = true): the substrate is a deterministic elastic system with no stochastic element.
2. No superdeterminism (MI = true by construction): the substrate's initial state is not correlated with measurement settings by assumption; the model explicitly rejects fine-tuning.
3. Local substrate (forces propagate at finite speed through the lattice): this is a stronger claim than just "local causality" — it rules out Bohmian guidance equations, which require instantaneous nonlocal coupling.

Under these three commitments, nonlocal hidden variables and superdeterminism are both excluded, leaving retrocausality as the unique remaining option. This is a stronger claim than "retrocausality is one option" and should be stated explicitly in the paper as a theorem.

The remaining gap is the Born rule: the model does not recover the Born probability rule from the deterministic substrate. This must be stated as an open problem rather than claimed.
