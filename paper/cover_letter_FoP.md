In the interest of full transparency: this is a **substantially revised and expanded version** of a manuscript previously submitted to *Foundations of Physics* and declined without external review [prior manuscript ID / approx. date]. I am resubmitting because the present version differs from the earlier one not in degree but in kind, and because I believe its scope and contribution now fall squarely within the journal's foundational remit. I would be grateful if you would consider whether it now merits external review.

**What the paper is.** It develops a deterministic, *substrate-first* foundational question: whether relativistic kinematics, gauge connections, and particle-like localization can arise as **emergent regularities** from a single underlying object — a 4D cubic brane lattice embedded with codimension zero in a flat 4D Euclidean ambient — rather than being postulated. The central modeling point is that the essential nonlinearity is **geometric** (it comes from the 4D Euclidean link length and the induced metric, not from an auxiliary field or hand-imposed thresholds), and that this same geometry couples transverse amplitude to in-brane contraction. The paper consolidates the lattice-to-continuum theory, identifies which bridges are already fixed by the model, and defines the decisive computations that would test its emergence claims. It closes with an interpretive stance dictated by Bell's theorem: causality as a property of soliton chirality and entanglement as the continuity of one branching 4D worldtube — a retrocausal completion in the family of the two-state-vector, transactional, and time-symmetric readings.

**What is new relative to the earlier, declined version.** The revision adds the substance that was missing:

- a concrete, **falsifiable numerical program** together with an implemented substrate solver and initial simulations (linear dispersion and isotropy of the substrate; construction and diagnostic decomposition of localized candidate modes);
- a **U(1) × SU(3) internal-sector analysis** in which an axis-aligned triplet on the cubic lattice yields, under inter-axis mixing, a non-Abelian U(3) transport problem with an SU(3)-like traceless part, and in which the prestress parameter α controls the sector split;
- the **retrocausal-worldtube interpretation of Bell** as an explicit, committed stance rather than a gesture;
- figures illustrating the theory structure, the constructed candidate mode, and the channel-decomposition diagnostic.

**On scope — and why I think this version is appropriate for review.** The manuscript is deliberately and explicitly *not* presented as a replacement for the Standard Model or general relativity, both of which it treats as empirically validated effective theories. It includes a dedicated **Non-claims** section that bounds the contribution precisely: it does *not* claim a derivation of the full gauge group or coupling constants, a Newtonian limit with a derived value of G, a completed Maxwell/Yang–Mills reduction, a finished proton or electron solution, or an identification of any specific topological object with a specific physical particle. The intended contribution is a **sharpened substrate model whose mathematical bridges are explicit enough to be attacked directly in simulation**, plus a concrete route for deciding whether its main emergence claims survive computation. I have tried to make the claims exactly as strong as the evidence supports, and no stronger.

I believe this honestly-scoped, computation-anchored framing is a good fit for *Foundations of Physics*, in the tradition of work that asks whether familiar physical structure can emerge from simpler deterministic ground ingredients.
