\section{Experimental setting}\label{experimental-setting}

\textbf{Note:} The following describes a proposed simulation architecture for testing the brane model. Implementation is ongoing and numerical results will be presented in future work. This section serves as a design specification outlining the computational framework, numerical methods, and falsifiable predictions that the model generates.

\subsection{Implementation Philosophy: Emergent vs. Explicit}\label{implementation-philosophy}

\textbf{Core principle:} The simulation integrates \emph{only} the microscopic
brane degrees of freedom (node positions and velocities) evolving according to
the discretized equations of motion derived from the action
\eqref{eq:brane-action}. All emergent quantities
— effective metric, electromagnetic fields, particle identities, measurement
outcomes, etc. — are computed diagnostically from $\mathbf X(x,t)$ and
$\dot{\mathbf X}(x,t)$ and are \emph{never} fed back into the equations of motion
as independent sources, forces, or constraints.

\paragraph{What is implemented (explicit in code):}
\begin{itemize}
\item The 4D embedding map $\mathbf{X}(x,t) \in \mathbb{R}^4$ discretized as node positions $\mathbf{R}_p(t)$
\item Elastic energy functionals: stretching $U_\text{str}$ and bending $U_\text{bend}$
\item Time evolution via velocity-Verlet integration: $\mathbf{F}_p = -\partial U/\partial \mathbf{R}_p$, $m_p \ddot{\mathbf{R}}_p = \mathbf{F}_p$
\item Boundary conditions (periodic, free, or clamped)
\item Initial conditions: Gaussian pulses, toroidal configurations, amplitude patterns
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What must emerge (detected via measurement, \emph{not} coded):}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Lorentz invariance:} Isotropic dispersion $\omega(|\mathbf{k}|)$ measured from wave propagation tests (Section~\ref{calibration-dispersion-isotropy})
\item \textbf{Gravity:} Induced metric $g_{ij} = \partial_i \mathbf{X} \cdot \partial_j \mathbf{X}$ computed post-hoc from node positions; geodesic deflection ray-traced through $g$ (E4, E6)
\item \textbf{Electromagnetism:} Coulomb-like $1/r^2$ field measured from time-averaged amplitude $\bar{X}^4(\mathbf{x})$ around stable toroidal solitons (E7)
\item \textbf{Nonlinear threshold:} Emergent energy density threshold for soliton nucleation (E1); \emph{not} imposed as a hard cutoff on amplitude or strain
\item \textbf{Particle stability:} Toroidal solitons that self-confine via nonlinear elasticity (E2); no explicit "particle glue" or confinement potential added
\item \textbf{Spinor behavior:} $4\pi$ holonomy measured from phase transport around rotated configurations (E3)
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{No back-reaction from emergent fields.}
Even when we visualise or analyse effective quantities such as $g_{ij}$,
Coulomb-like fields, or reconstructed wavefunctions, these objects remain purely
diagnostic. In the implementation they are read out from the brane state for
measurement and comparison with continuum theories; they never act back on the
brane as additional forces or constraints. This ensures that any agreement with
relativity, electromagnetism or quantum statistics is genuinely \emph{emergent}
from the substrate dynamics.

\paragraph{Photon field initialization (clarification):}
When we refer to "implementing a photon field component" for the electron model (e.g., E2, E7), this means \emph{initializing} the brane with a photon-like wave pattern confined in a toroidal structure—a specific choice of $\mathbf{R}_p(t=0)$ and $\mathbf{v}_p(t=0)$. The subsequent time evolution is governed \emph{solely} by the elastic forces $\mathbf{F}_p = -\partial U/\partial \mathbf{R}_p$. There is \textbf{no electromagnetic force} $\mathbf{F}_\text{EM}$ added to the equations of motion; any EM-like behavior (field gradients, Coulomb scaling) must appear naturally in the resulting amplitude distribution $\bar{X}^4(\mathbf{x})$.

\paragraph{Experimental workflow:}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Set initial conditions $\{\mathbf{R}_p(0), \mathbf{v}_p(0)\}$ representing a physical scenario (e.g., localized pulse, toroidal electron, point mass)
\item Evolve forward using only elastic forces from $U_\text{str} + U_\text{bend}$
\item \textbf{Measure} emergent quantities: dispersion $\omega(\mathbf{k})$, induced metric $g_{ij}$, energy localization, field profiles $\bar{X}^4(\mathbf{x})$, geodesic deflection angles, etc.
\item Compare measured behavior against known physics (Lorentz cones, $1/r$ potentials, $1/r^2$ fields) to assess whether the substrate dynamics reproduce standard results
\end{enumerate}

This approach ensures that the simulation tests the \emph{sufficiency} of geometric elasticity to reproduce observed physics, rather than presupposing the structure of electromagnetism, gravity, or quantum thresholds.

\paragraph{No feedback from emergent fields (architectural constraint).}
For clarity, we emphasize a strict one-way flow of information in the
implementation. The microscopic solver evolves only the brane embedding
$\mathbf{X}(x,t)$ under the elastic energy functionals described in
Sec.~\ref{discrete-energies-and-forces}, using a fixed integration scheme and
boundary conditions. All effective fields --- electromagnetic
potentials and fields, induced metrics, gravitational potentials, and
Lorentz-force-like terms --- are defined purely as \emph{diagnostic
functionals} of $\mathbf{X}$ and its derivatives, evaluated on
snapshots of the simulation. They are never fed back into the
microscopic equations of motion as additional forces, constraints, or
dimension-specific damping terms. In particular, the fourth (normal)
component $X^4$ enters the dynamics only through the mechanical
Lagrangian; any identification of $X^4$ with an effective charge or
scalar potential is an interpretational step applied \emph{after} the
fact. This architectural separation ensures that the numerical
experiments genuinely test whether the simple brane dynamics are
\emph{sufficient} to reproduce the observed structures of relativity,
electromagnetism, and gravity.

\subsection{Measurement Procedures for Emergent Fields}\label{measurement-procedures}

A key feature of this framework is that electromagnetic and gravitational fields are not fundamental inputs but emerge from the geometric configuration of the brane. This raises a methodological question: how do we operationally measure these fields in the simulation?

\subsubsection{Measuring the electromagnetic field}

In the brane picture, the effective electrostatic potential $\Phi_{\text{EM}}(\mathbf{x})$ is defined in terms of the coarse-grained amplitude displacement $\bar{X}^4(\mathbf{x})$ (see Sec.~\ref{sec:charge-amplitude}),
\begin{equation}
  \Phi_{\text{EM}}(\mathbf{x}) \;=\; \kappa_{\Phi} \,\bar{X}^4(\mathbf{x}),
  \label{eq:Phi-EM-measurement}
\end{equation}
where $\kappa_{\Phi}$ is a phenomenological coupling constant. The measurement procedure is:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Extract fourth component:} From the node positions $\mathbf{R}_p(t)$, extract the fourth (normal) component $X^4_p(t) \equiv [\mathbf{R}_p(t)]_4$.
  \item \textbf{Time-average:} Compute the time-averaged amplitude displacement over a window $\Delta t_{\text{avg}}$ that is long compared to the internal Compton oscillation period $2\pi/\omega_C$ but short compared to the timescale of macroscopic changes,
    \[
      \bar{X}^4_p \;=\; \frac{1}{\Delta t_{\text{avg}}} \int_{t_0}^{t_0 + \Delta t_{\text{avg}}} X^4_p(t')\, dt'.
    \]
    This procedure filters out bound, oscillatory contributions and isolates the net monopole component of the amplitude deformation.
  \item \textbf{Spatial interpolation:} Interpolate the discrete values $\{\bar{X}^4_p\}$ onto a regular spatial grid using a smooth kernel (e.g., cubic spline) to obtain the continuous field $\bar{X}^4(\mathbf{x})$.
  \item \textbf{Compute potential and field:} Define the effective electrostatic potential via Eq.~\eqref{eq:Phi-EM-measurement} and compute the electric field as
    \[
      \mathbf{E}(\mathbf{x}) \;=\; -\nabla \Phi_{\text{EM}}(\mathbf{x}) \;=\; -\kappa_{\Phi} \nabla \bar{X}^4(\mathbf{x}),
    \]
    using numerical differentiation (e.g., central differences) on the interpolated grid.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Epistemic status.}
The coupling constant $\kappa_{\Phi}$ is not derived from first principles in the current work but is treated as a phenomenological parameter that sets the scale of the electrostatic interaction. In a complete microscopic theory it would be determined by the brane's elastic response to amplitude deformations. For the purposes of the numerical experiments, $\kappa_{\Phi}$ is chosen such that the measured far-field $\mathbf{E}$-field of a toroidal electron soliton reproduces the known Coulomb magnitude $e/(4\pi\varepsilon_0 r^2)$ at distances $r \gg \lambda_C$.

\subsubsection{Measuring the gravitational field}

In the brane picture, gravitational effects arise from the curvature of the induced metric $g_{ij}(\mathbf{x})$ on the brane (see Sec.~\ref{subsec:weak_field_gravity}). The measurement procedure is:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Compute tangent vectors:} At each material point $x_p$ in the simulation domain, estimate the tangent vectors $\partial_i \mathbf{X}$ using finite differences in material coordinates,
    \[
      \partial_i \mathbf{X}|_{x_p} \;\approx\; \frac{\mathbf{R}_{p+\hat{i}} - \mathbf{R}_{p-\hat{i}}}{2h},
      \qquad i=1,2,3,
    \]
    where $\hat{i}$ denotes a unit step in the $i$-th material direction and $h$ is the lattice spacing.
  \item \textbf{Build induced metric:} Compute the spatial components of the induced metric as
    \[
      g_{ij}(\mathbf{x}_p) \;=\; \partial_i \mathbf{X}|_{x_p} \cdot \partial_j \mathbf{X}|_{x_p},
      \qquad i,j=1,2,3.
    \]
    In the weak-field regime this reduces to $g_{ij} \approx \delta_{ij} + h_{ij}$, where $h_{ij}$ is a small perturbation related to the amplitude gradients (Eq.~\eqref{eq:PhiG_from_gradX4}).
  \item \textbf{Extract effective potential:} In the static, weak-field limit the effective gravitational potential $\Phi_{\text{grav}}(\mathbf{x})$ can be extracted from the time-time component of the effective spacetime metric,
    \[
      g_{00}(\mathbf{x}) \;\approx\; -\left(1 + \frac{2\Phi_{\text{grav}}(\mathbf{x})}{c^2}\right),
    \]
    which in the present framework is built from the quadratic combination of amplitude gradients postulated in Sec.~\ref{subsec:weak_field_gravity}. For spherically symmetric configurations, $\Phi_{\text{grav}}(r)$ can also be computed from the spatial metric components by solving the weak-field Einstein equations or by matching to the Newtonian limit.
  \item \textbf{Ray-trace geodesics:} To measure gravitational light bending, initialize a null geodesic (probe photon trajectory) with initial position $\mathbf{x}_0$ and direction $\hat{\mathbf{k}}_0$ and numerically integrate the geodesic equation
    \[
      \frac{d^2 x^\mu}{d\lambda^2} + \Gamma^\mu_{\nu\rho} \frac{dx^\nu}{d\lambda} \frac{dx^\rho}{d\lambda} = 0,
    \]
    where $\Gamma^\mu_{\nu\rho}$ are the Christoffel symbols computed from $g_{\mu\nu}$ and $\lambda$ is an affine parameter along the geodesic. The deflection angle $\delta\theta$ is the angular difference between the initial and final asymptotic directions of the ray.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Epistemic status.}
The metric ansatz $g_{\mu\nu}[\mathbf{X}]$ introduced in Sec.~\ref{subsec:weak_field_gravity} is phenomenological rather than derived from a systematic expansion of the brane Lagrangian. The measurement procedure described here tests whether this ansatz, when applied to the simulated brane configurations, reproduces the known weak-field predictions of general relativity (e.g., $\Phi_{\text{grav}}(r) \propto -G M/r$ and the Newtonian deflection angle $\delta\theta \approx 2GM/(c^2 b)$). Discrepancies would indicate either that the metric ansatz must be refined or that additional terms in the brane Lagrangian are needed.

\paragraph{Summary: measurement as coarse-graining.}
In both cases—electromagnetic and gravitational—the measurement procedures are coarse-graining operations that map the microscopic brane configuration $\{\mathbf{R}_p(t)\}$ to effective fields $\Phi_{\text{EM}}(\mathbf{x})$, $\mathbf{E}(\mathbf{x})$, $g_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf{x})$ living on the emergent spacetime. These procedures are not arbitrary: they implement the constitutive relations postulated in the theoretical sections (Sec.~\ref{sec:charge-amplitude} and Sec.~\ref{subsec:weak_field_gravity}). The experiments test whether these constitutive relations, when applied to self-consistent brane configurations (toroidal solitons, localized bulges), yield the correct large-distance behavior ($1/r^2$ Coulomb field, $1/r$ Newtonian potential) without fine-tuning of the microscopic parameters beyond the basic matching conditions of Sec.~\ref{sec:param-matching}.

\subsection{Discrete Simulation Framework}\label{discrete-simulation-framework}

We discretize the material domain $\Omega$ by nodes $x_p\in\Omega$ and store
\begin{equation}\label{eq:discrete-embedding}
  \mathbf R_p(t) \approx \mathbf X(x_p,t)\in\mathbb R^4,
\end{equation}
so that the discrete configuration $\{\mathbf R_p\}$ is a sampling of the continuum embedding
$\mathbf X(x,t)$ introduced in Section~\ref{sec:conceptual-model}.
The fourth component of $\mathbf R_p$ encodes the out-of-brane displacement; there is no separate amplitude field.

The choice of continuum parameters $(\rho_m,T,\kappa)$
is constrained by the parameter-matching conditions of
Section~\ref{sec:param-matching}; in particular, the amplitude scale calibration
of Section~\ref{amplitude-scale-calibration} implements the matching to
$(m_e,c,\hbar,e)$ at the level of the discrete lattice.


\subsubsection{Lattice and neighborhoods}\label{lattice-and-neighborhoods}

\begin{itemize}
\item Use an \textbf{FCC lattice} in material space (spacing $h$), giving 12 equal-length nearest neighbors $\mathcal N(p)$ for good isotropy.
\item If FCC is impractical, a cubic 26-stencil with \textbf{isotropic weights} (4th-order Laplacian fit) can be used; document the weights.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Discrete energies and forces}\label{discrete-energies-and-forces}

We approximate the continuum energy in terms of \textbf{edge stretches} (intrinsic strain) and \textbf{bending} between neighboring edge directions.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Stretching energy (Hooke-like):}
  \[
  U_\text{str}=\sum_{(p,q)\in\mathcal E}
  \phi\left(\frac{|\mathbf R_q-\mathbf R_p|-h}{h}\right),
  \]
  with a simple quadratic potential
  \[
  \phi(\epsilon)=\frac{1}{2}k\,\epsilon^2,
  \]
  so that the discrete stretching law reduces locally to a
  Hooke-like response. In the present work we do not include
  any additional material saturation; all nonlinear behavior
  arises from the geometric dependence of distances and curvature
  on the brane embedding.

\paragraph{Nonlinearity mode (implementation note).}

In code we distinguish multiple nonlinearity modes via a
\texttt{BraneNonlinearityMode} enumeration:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{NONE}: Linearized small-displacement approximation;
        edge stiffness computed using $\epsilon \approx
        (\partial_i\xi_j)/h$ rather than the full 4D Euclidean
        distance.
  \item \texttt{PURE\_GEOMETRY}: Default. Each edge stiffness is
        computed from the exact 4D Euclidean distance
        $\ell=|\mathbf R_q-\mathbf R_p|$, so lateral contraction from
        normal displacements arises purely from metric changes. This is
        the primary mode for all emergent-field experiments.
  \item \texttt{SCHWINGER\_LIMIT}, \texttt{HUYGENS\_ELASTIC},
        \texttt{HIGGS\_MEXICAN\_HAT}, \texttt{CUSTOM}: Optional
        exploratory extensions that introduce additional strong-field
        or material-nonlinearity potentials beyond the purely geometric
        effect. These are not required for the core model and should be
        viewed as experimental overlays for probing robustness and
        strong-field regimes.
\end{itemize}
Unless otherwise stated, all experiments use
\texttt{PURE\_GEOMETRY}. The mathematical model in
Section~\ref{conceptual-model} corresponds exactly to this mode.

  The derivative (useful for force implementation) is
  \[
  \phi'(\epsilon) = k\epsilon.
  \]

\item \textbf{Bending/curvature energy (optional but stabilizing):}
  For each node $p$, penalize changes of edge directions:
  \[
  U_\text{bend}=\frac{\kappa}{2}\sum_{p}\sum_{q<r\in\mathcal N(p)}
  \big(1-\hat e_{pq}\cdot\hat e_{pr}\big),
  \quad \hat e_{pq}=\frac{\mathbf R_q-\mathbf R_p}{|\mathbf R_q-\mathbf R_p|}.
  \]
  For small angles between neighboring edges, $1-\hat e_{pq}\cdot\hat e_{pr}\approx \tfrac12\theta^2$.
  Thus $U_\text{bend}$ acts as a discrete approximation to the squared mean curvature $|b|^2$ of the
  embedding, consistent with the continuum brane picture. This helps maintain coherent toroidal shapes.

\item \textbf{Total energy:} $U=U_\text{str}+U_\text{bend}$.
\end{itemize}

Forces are $\mathbf F_p=-\partial U/\partial \mathbf R_p$. Because $|\mathbf R_q-\mathbf R_p|$ is a \textbf{4D norm}, any deformation in the 4th direction automatically couples to in-brane edge lengths, producing the desired \textbf{geometric lateral contraction}.

\paragraph{Discrete-to-continuum parameter mapping}\label{discrete-continuum-mapping}

The continuum parameters introduced in Section~\ref{conceptual-model} relate to the discrete simulation as follows. Each lattice node represents a material volume element of size $h^3$, where $h$ is the lattice spacing (edge length in the reference FCC configuration). The node mass is $m_p = \rho_m h^3$, with $\rho_m$ the continuum mass density of the brane substrate. The effective \textbf{isotropic tension} $T$ in the continuum is encoded in the discrete spring stiffness parameter $k$ appearing in $\phi(\epsilon)$; in the small-strain limit, dimensional analysis and dispersion measurements yield $T \sim k/h$. The \textbf{bending stiffness} $\kappa$ in the continuum curvature energy $\tfrac{\kappa}{2}|b|^2$ maps to the discrete parameter $\kappa$ in $U_\text{bend}$, with units adjusted by powers of $h$ to maintain dimensional consistency. Finally, the shear modulus $\mu$ in the continuum description can be introduced via additional discrete angular or shear springs if needed, though for simplicity we often set $\mu\to 0$ in the isotropic limit. These mappings are validated by measuring the wave speed $c = \sqrt{T/\rho_m}$ from numerical dispersion tests and verifying consistency with the continuum prediction (see Section~\ref{calibration-dispersion-isotropy}).

\subsubsection{Amplitude scale calibration}\label{amplitude-scale-calibration}

To instantiate the brane in physical units we must fix not only the wave speed
$c$ but also the absolute scale of normal displacements $X^4$, which has the
same dimension of length as the lateral coordinates. Two complementary routes
are used to estimate the amplitude scale $A$ of microscopic oscillations.

\paragraph{Route (i): Compton-cell mass calibration.}
We first tie the bulk mechanical parameters to the electron rest mass and the
reduced Compton wavelength
$\lambda_C = \hbar/(m_e c)$. In the continuum, a small-amplitude transverse wave
with typical amplitude $A$, angular frequency $\omega$ and effective volume
$V_{\text{eff}}$ has elastic energy
\begin{equation}
  E_{\text{wave}} \;\sim\; \frac{1}{2}\,\rho\,A^2\,\omega^2\,V_{\text{eff}},
  \label{eq:wave-energy-amplitude}
\end{equation}
where $\rho$ is the brane mass density. As a simple calibration ansatz we assume
that a Compton-scale ``cell'' of the brane carries roughly one electron mass,
\begin{equation}
  \rho\,\lambda_C^3 \;\approx\; m_e,
\end{equation}
which fixes $\rho$ and, via $c^2 = K/\rho$, the bulk modulus $K$. For a mode
with $\omega \approx 2\pi c/\lambda_C$ localized in a volume
$V_{\text{eff}}\sim\lambda_C^3$, imposing that this configuration represents
a single quantum with energy $E_{\text{wave}}\approx\hbar\omega$ yields an
amplitude scale $A=\mathcal{O}(\lambda_C)$; a simple estimate gives
$A \approx \lambda_C/\sqrt{\pi}$ up to order-unity geometric factors. This
choice sets the overall substrate mechanical scale (via $\rho$ and $K$) in terms
of $(m_e,c,\hbar)$.

\paragraph{Route (ii): Charge and internal energy density.}
Independently, Section~\ref{sec:charge-magnitude} relates the observed charge
magnitude $|e|$ to the internal energy density and geometry of a Compton-scale
toroidal soliton. There the same displacement field $X^4(\mathbf{x},t)$ is used
to compute an elastic energy density $\mathcal{E}(\xi,\partial\xi)$ in J/m$^3$,
which is required to integrate to $m_e c^2$ over a toroidal volume, while the
far-field gradient of the time-averaged deformation reproduces a Coulomb
potential with $q\approx e$. This construction fixes another combination of the
mechanical parameters and the typical internal amplitude $A$ of the soliton.
Within the uncertainties of the torus geometry, the resulting $A$ is again of
order $\lambda_C$, in agreement with the Compton-cell calibration above.
In particular, the single-quantum amplitude scale $A$ obtained here
enters directly into the geometric nonlinearity parameter
$\eta$ defined in Sec.~\ref{threshold-localization}. For modes with
wavelengths of order the Compton wavelength $\lambda_C$, this parameter
satisfies $\eta = \mathcal O(1)$: the same calibration that ties the
substrate to $(m_e,c,\hbar)$ therefore also marks the onset of strong
geometric nonlinearity for photon-like excitations.

\paragraph{Practical simulation choice.}
In the discrete code we select a lattice spacing $h$ and identify each node with
a material volume $h^3$ and mass $m_p = \rho h^3$. Given a target amplitude
scale $A$ from either route, a ``one-quantum'' initialization of a photon-like
packet or a toroidal electron is implemented by choosing discrete displacements
$[R_p]_4$ such that the discrete elastic energy
$\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_p m_p |\dot{R}_{p,4}|^2 + U_{\text{elastic}}(\{\mathbf R_p\})$
matches $\hbar\omega$ (for a photon) or $m_e c^2$ (for an electron) to within a
specified tolerance. Both calibration routes therefore determine not only the
mechanical parameters $(\rho,K)$ but also the physically meaningful range of
normal displacements $X^4$ to be used when setting up initial conditions in the
experiments of Section~\ref{experiments}.

\subsubsection{Time integration and stability}\label{time-integration-and-stability}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Integrator:} velocity-Verlet (leapfrog), symplectic \& time-reversible:
  \begin{align*}
  \mathbf v^{n+1/2}&=\mathbf v^n+\tfrac{\Delta t}{2}\mathbf a^n,\\
  \mathbf R^{n+1}&=\mathbf R^n+\Delta t\mathbf v^{n+1/2},\\
  \mathbf v^{n+1}&=\mathbf v^{n+1/2}+\tfrac{\Delta t}{2}\mathbf a^{n+1}.
  \end{align*}
\item \textbf{CFL guideline (linear regime):} $\Delta t<\eta h/c$ with empirical $\eta\approx0.33$ for FCC. Here $c$ is measured from dispersion tests (Section~\ref{calibration-dispersion-isotropy}) rather than assumed from continuum $c=\sqrt{T/\rho}$. Document the chosen $\eta$ and measured $c$.
\item \textbf{Energy tracking:} $E_\text{tot}=K+U$ with $K=\tfrac12\sum_p \rho h^3|\dot{\mathbf R}_p|^2$.
  Here $\rho$ is the continuum mass density and $h^3$ is the material volume represented by a node,
  so that each node effectively carries mass $m_p=\rho h^3$.
  Report drift (target $<10^{-5}$ over long runs, periodic BCs).
\item \textbf{Implementation note:} No explicit amplitude or strain clamps are applied during time integration. All nonlinear confinement effects arise from the geometric dependence of edge lengths on the 4D embedding. The integrator advances $\mathbf R_p$ and $\mathbf v_p$ based solely on forces $\mathbf F_p = -\partial U/\partial \mathbf R_p$ computed from $U_\text{str} + U_\text{bend}$, ensuring that any emergent localization or threshold behavior arises from the elastic dynamics rather than from artificial hard cutoffs.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Boundary conditions}\label{boundary-conditions}

In the discrete simulations we must close the brane patch by choosing
boundary conditions that specify how the medium interacts with the
“outside world”. In practice we use:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Periodic} (default numerics): the simulation domain is
  wrapped onto a torus so that waves leaving one side re-enter on the
  opposite side. This mimics a locally homogeneous, effectively infinite
  brane patch.
\item \textbf{Free} ($\partial_n \mathbf X$ traction-free in the in-brane
  sense): the brane is not anchored at the boundary, allowing transverse
  motion without external forces; this approximates an isolated finite
  membrane embedded in a larger medium.
\item \textbf{Clamped} ($\mathbf X=\mathbf X_0$ on $\partial\Omega$):
  the boundary is rigidly fixed, representing attachment to an external
  structure and suppressing fluctuations at the edge.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Induced metric and geodesics (from data)}\label{induced-metric-and-geodesics}

Given positions $\mathbf R_p$, estimate discrete tangents $\partial_i\mathbf X$ via central differences in material space, build $g_{ij}=\partial_i\mathbf X\cdot\partial_j\mathbf X$, and ray-trace probe signals using $ds^2=-c^2dt^2+g_{ij}dx^i dx^j$. This quantifies "gravitational" deflection due to 4D bulges.

\subsubsection{Calibration: dispersion \& isotropy (emergent relativity test)}\label{calibration-dispersion-isotropy}

Initialize small-amplitude plane waves $\mathbf R_p=\mathbf R^0_p+\boldsymbol\xi\cos(\mathbf k\cdot x_p)$ in three non-collinear $\hat k$ and \emph{numerically measure} $\omega(\mathbf k)$ from the time evolution. Verify that the measured dispersion follows
\[
\omega \approx c|\mathbf k|,\qquad
\text{relative error } \le 1\% \text{ up to } kh\approx0.5.
\]
This demonstrates isotropic cones for internal observers in the linear regime.

\subsubsection{Experiments}\label{experiments}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{E1 — Nonlinear localization near the elastic threshold:}
  Launch a Gaussian disturbance with compact support and varying peak
  amplitude, scanning across the onset of the geometric nonlinear regime.
  Track the time evolution of the
  energy density, center-of-energy position, and localization
  functionals as a function of total energy and peak amplitude.

  The goal is to determine whether the geometric nonlinearity alone
  admits long-lived, self-localized lumps and whether there exists
  an emergent threshold in energy density for such localization.
  Diagnostics: peak amplitude, energy localization, lifetime.

\item \textbf{E2 — Toroidal soliton (electron topology):} Seed a torus in 4D—i.e., initialize $\mathbf X$ so that the set of nodes with elevated normal offset forms a looped tube (major radius $R$, minor $r$). Add a compatible tangential phase pattern (as initial velocities) around the loop. Track confinement ratio, torus symmetry, robustness to perturbations.

  The experiment probes whether the geometric nonlinearity of the
  medium and the toroidal topology are sufficient to yield an emergent,
  dynamically maintained soliton, without imposing any additional
  strong-field cutoff or pair-creation rule.

\item \textbf{E3 — Spinor holonomy ($4\pi$ test):} Rigidly rotate the toroidal configuration in 4D by $\Delta\theta$ while adiabatically transporting its internal phase marker (defined from a local orthonormal frame along the loop). Expect sign flip after $2\pi$, recovery after $4\pi$.

\item \textbf{E4 — Geodesic bending:} Place a weak probe packet far from a localized bulge and ray-trace through $g$. Measure deflection vs. curvature intensity.

\item \textbf{E5 — Emergent kinematics:} Propagate compact, small-amplitude packets; verify constant group speed $c$ and angular isotropy; quantify deviations as amplitude increases and geometric nonlinearity becomes significant.
  Any breakdown of effective Lorentz invariance is treated as an emergent phenomenon of the medium.

\item \textbf{E6 — Newtonian gravity recovery:} Place a localized, spherically symmetric amplitude bulge (representing a mass concentration) at rest in the brane. Initialize it with total energy $E_{\text{bulge}} \approx m_{\text{bulge}} c^2$ distributed over a compact volume $V_{\text{bulge}} \sim \lambda_{\text{bulge}}^3$. Compute the induced metric $g_{ij}$ by measuring local tangent vectors $\partial_i \mathbf{X}$ on a spherical shell at various radii $r$ from the bulge center. Extract the effective gravitational potential $\Phi_{\text{grav}}(\mathbf{x})$ from the effective emergent metric component $g_{00} \approx -(1 + 2\Phi_{\text{grav}}/c^2)$ and verify:
\begin{itemize}
\item At distances $r \gg \lambda_{\text{bulge}}$, the potential should approach $\Phi_{\text{grav}}(r) \propto -G m_{\text{bulge}}/r$ (inverse-square law).
\item The effective gravitational constant $G_{\text{eff}}$ should be consistent across different bulge masses and probe distances.
\item Ray-trace null geodesics past the bulge to measure light deflection and compare with the Newtonian prediction $\delta\theta \approx 2G m_{\text{bulge}}/(c^2 b)$ for impact parameter $b$.
\end{itemize}
\emph{Success criterion:} If the simulation reproduces $\Phi \propto -1/r$ with deviation $< 5\%$ over the range $2\lambda_{\text{bulge}} < r < 10\lambda_{\text{bulge}}$, this constitutes evidence that the brane framework can recover Newtonian gravity in the weak-field, static limit. Further tests with rotating or highly concentrated bulges would be needed to approach the Schwarzschild or Kerr solutions of general relativity.

\item \textbf{E7 — Charge field of toroidal electron:} Initialize a stable toroidal soliton (as in E2) representing an electron with internal circulation defining chirality $\chi = +1$. Measure the coarse-grained amplitude displacement $\bar{X}^4(\mathbf{x})$ on a spherical shell of radius $r$ around the torus center by time-averaging $X^4(\mathbf{x}, t)$ over multiple internal oscillation periods (timescale $\sim 1/\omega_{\text{Compton}}$). Compute the effective scalar potential $\Phi(\mathbf{x}) = \kappa \bar{X}^4(\mathbf{x})$ and the resulting electric field $\mathbf{E}(\mathbf{x}) = -\nabla\Phi(\mathbf{x})$. Verify:
\begin{itemize}
\item Spherical symmetry: $|\mathbf{E}(r)|$ should depend only on radial distance $r$ for $r \gg \lambda_C$.
\item Coulomb behavior: At large distances, $|\mathbf{E}(r)| \propto 1/r^2$ with relative deviation $< 10\%$ over $2\lambda_C < r < 10\lambda_C$.
\item Sign reversal: Repeat with $\chi = -1$ (positron); verify $\Phi$ and $\mathbf{E}$ flip sign while $|\mathbf{E}(r)|$ remains unchanged.
\item Near-field structure: Close to the torus ($r \lesssim \lambda_C$), document deviations from Coulomb law due to finite soliton size and internal structure.
\end{itemize}
\emph{Success criterion:} If the far-field $\mathbf{E}$-field reproduces $1/r^2$ scaling and reverses sign with chirality, this confirms that charge emerges as a signed amplitude deformation, as postulated in Sec.~\ref{sec:charge-amplitude}. Deviations in the near field would reveal the extended structure of the electron and provide testable predictions distinct from point-particle QED.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Falsifiable criteria}\label{falsifiable-criteria}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Relativistic cones:} dispersion isotropy $\le 1\%$ up to $kh\approx0.5$.
\item \textbf{Holonomy:} internal phase shows $4\pi$ periodicity under $2\pi$ rigid rotation.
\item \textbf{Emergent threshold:} persistent localization appears only when the integrated energy density satisfies $\langle\mathcal E\rangle_{V_c}V_c\ge m_ec^2$. This threshold arises from the nonlinear elastic dynamics of the medium, not from an imposed strong-field cutoff or amplitude clamp.
\item \textbf{Metric bending:} ray deflection scales with curvature extracted from $g$.
\item \textbf{Newtonian gravity:} spherically symmetric mass produces potential $\Phi_{\text{grav}}(r) \propto -1/r$ with deviation $< 5\%$ over range $2\lambda_{\text{bulge}} < r < 10\lambda_{\text{bulge}}$.
\item \textbf{Coulomb charge field:} toroidal soliton produces spherically symmetric electric field $|\mathbf{E}(r)| \propto 1/r^2$ with deviation $< 10\%$ over range $2\lambda_C < r < 10\lambda_C$; field reverses sign with chirality $\chi$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Interpretation layers (clarity note)}\label{interpretation-layers}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Substrate:} a single geometric object $\mathbf X(x,t)\in\mathbb R^4$ with dynamics from $\mathcal L(\mathbf X,\partial\mathbf X)$.
\item \textbf{Effective physics for internal observers:} built from the induced metric $g_{ij}$ and wave kinematics on the brane; no extra fields are postulated. The familiar EM interpretation can be treated phenomenologically elsewhere; it is not required here.
\item \textbf{Threshold phenomena:} The localization thresholds and confinement scales discussed in this work (e.g., the energy density criterion $\langle\mathcal E\rangle_{V_c}V_c \ge m_e c^2$) are not imposed as external cutoffs or quantum rules, but emerge dynamically from the geometric nonlinearity of the medium. The simulations test whether this purely geometric-tensional framework is sufficient to produce soliton stability and pair confinement without additional ad-hoc mechanisms.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Note on implementation:} The discrete simulation framework described in Section~\ref{discrete-simulation-framework} supersedes earlier spring-lattice prototypes. Those preliminary implementations used a simpler cubic lattice with Hooke's-law springs and Euler integration; the current framework uses an FCC lattice with geometric nonlinearity and velocity-Verlet integration, providing better isotropy and numerical stability.
