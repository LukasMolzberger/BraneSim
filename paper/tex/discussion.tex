\section{Discussion}\label{discussion}

This model offers a radical reinterpretation of core physical concepts
by shifting the ontological basis of physics from abstract mathematical
fields to a concrete, tensioned medium --- a 3D brane embedded in 4D
space. By doing so, it challenges the standard partitioning of physics
into separate frameworks for general relativity, quantum mechanics, and
field theory, and instead suggests that all three may be different
aspects of wave propagation and internal tension dynamics within a
unified medium.

The strength of this approach lies in its coherence and minimalism:
rather than postulating multiple fundamental fields, point particles, or
probabilistic axioms, it reduces the complexity to a single substrate
with consistent rules. Properties like mass, spin, and even gauge
symmetry emerge not through external prescriptions but through the
solitonic geometry and oscillatory modes of the medium. Gravitation
becomes a local contraction effect tied to amplitude deformation, while
magnetic fields arise from relativistic reinterpretations of electric
fields --- reinforcing the idea that secondary forces may reflect
higher-order dynamical effects of a more fundamental structure.

Several parallels with analog gravity systems, such as Bose-Einstein
condensates and fluid simulations, suggest that emergent relativity from
wave propagation is not a mere metaphor, but a physically plausible
mechanism. Furthermore, the topological analogies to Skyrmions and Hopf
solitons provide grounding for the idea that stable particles can arise
as localized, non-dissipative excitations with internal symmetry and
spin structure.

From this vantage point, matter and radiation differ only in how the
underlying energy is organized: solitons correspond to ``canned''
energy, confined and stabilized by the brane's tension and nonlinear
self-interaction, whereas free photon modes correspond to ``released''
energy propagating as nearly linear waves. This echoes van der Mark and
't~Hooft's characterization of matter as ``canned energy'' and radiation
as ``free energy'' in their analysis of mass and light
\cite{vanderMark2000LightHeavy}, but here that slogan is given a concrete
mechanical realization in terms of explicit brane deformations.

\paragraph{Free versus bound charge in the brane picture.}

Throughout this work we have interpreted the brane's displacement along
the fourth, ``amplitude'' direction as encoding an effective electric
charge content of the medium. It is, however, important to distinguish
between \emph{bound} and \emph{free} contributions to this charge
content. The fast, structured internal photon circulating within the
toroidal electron soliton produces large, rapidly varying gradients and
curvature of $X^4(x,t)$; in analogy with polarization in dielectrics,
these contributions are most naturally interpreted as bound charge
patterns of the brane.
In the tubular-coordinate description of Section~\ref{subsec:tubular-electron}
this internal photon-like mode appears as a structured amplitude field
$\xi(t,z,x,y)$ in a curved world-tube, whose steep longitudinal
gradients are geometrically coupled to lateral deformations of the brane.
This provides a concrete mechanism for generating bound-charge patterns
that largely cancel at Compton scales while leaving a slowly varying
monopole component encoded in the coarse-grained field $\bar X^4(\mathbf x)$. On scales larger than the internal Compton
wavelength, the associated multipole moments largely cancel, so that
only a residual monopole component remains. This slowly varying,
non-cancelling part of the amplitude deformation is encoded in the
effective field $\bar{X}^4(\mathbf{x})$ and gives rise to the net free
charge density $\rho_{\text{free}}(\mathbf{x})$ that appears in the
Poisson equation~\eqref{eq:poisson-from-brane}. In this sense the
electron's observed electric charge is not tied to the instantaneous
value of $X^4$ at a point, but to the monopole component of its
curvature pattern on the brane after bound, oscillatory structures have
been taken into account. Pure radiation modes in the linear regime,
including external photon wave packets propagating on the brane, can
then be understood as oscillations of bound charge content with
vanishing net free charge: they strongly excite $X^4$ locally, but their
charge multipole expansion has no monopole term and thus do not
source a long-range Coulomb field.

However, the model also raises open questions and challenges. The framework now possesses a rigorous variational formulation grounded in a Lagrangian and action principle (Section~\ref{conceptual-model}), which provides the theoretical foundation for the continuum dynamics. Key open problems include:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Coupling of brane geometry to electromagnetism}: The four-potential $A_\mu$ is currently introduced phenomenologically on the emergent spacetime; a full derivation from the embedding field $\mathbf{X}$ remains to be developed.
\item \textbf{Recovery of Newtonian gravity and GR limits}: While geodesic focusing in the induced metric $g_{ij}$ provides a mechanism for gravitational effects, explicit demonstration of inverse-square potentials or Schwarzschild-like metrics in simple configurations is needed.
\item \textbf{Multi-particle quantum statistics and entanglement}: The model's deterministic, local-medium basis does not currently reproduce entanglement correlations or Bell inequality violations as observed experimentally. This represents a fundamental open problem: the model has no mechanism to generate the nonlocal correlations that are central to quantum mechanics. We explicitly acknowledge this conflict with experimental quantum mechanics and currently do not have a resolution.
\item \textbf{Connection to QED and renormalization}: The relationship between the extended solitonic electron structure and standard QED predictions (radiative corrections, running coupling, $g-2$ anomaly) requires clarification.
\end{itemize}

While not yet a full theory, this work constitutes a philosophical and
mathematical prototype --- a sandbox in which gravity, quantum behavior,
and particle identity emerge from the interplay of wave dynamics,
tension, and geometry. Its value may lie as much in the questions it
inspires as in the answers it attempts to provide. Further refinement
and critical engagement from both theorists and computational physicists
may help clarify whether this direction offers a fruitful path toward a
more intuitive and unified understanding of the physical world.
