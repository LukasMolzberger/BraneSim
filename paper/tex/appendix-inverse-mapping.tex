\section{Inverse mapping from guided electromagnetic modes to brane initial data}
\label{app:inverse-em-mapping}

In the main text, the electromagnetic sector is constructed in a
\emph{forward} direction: coarse-grained amplitude deformations of the
brane substrate source an effective scalar potential
$\Phi(\mathbf x) = \kappa\,\bar X^4(\mathbf x)$
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:phi-from-xi4}), which in the static far field obeys a
Poisson equation~\eqref{eq:poisson-from-brane} and is then promoted to
the time component of a four-potential $A_\mu$ in the Maxwell-type
wave equation~\eqref{eq:maxwell-wave}. In that construction the spatial
components of $A_\mu$ and the effective current are introduced
phenomenologically, and the brane $\to$ EM map is regarded as primary.

For the purposes of initializing numerical experiments with guided
photon modes (both in a straight waveguide and on the toroidal strip of
Appendix~\ref{app:tubular-electron}) it is useful to have a
\emph{restricted inverse mapping} in the opposite direction: a recipe
that takes a given classical guided mode
$(\mathbf E,\mathbf B)$ in an effective medium and constructs
corresponding microscopic initial data for the brane embedding
$\mathbf X(x,t)$. This appendix spells out such a mapping for a single,
monochromatic guided photon mode. It is \emph{not} intended as a general
inversion of Maxwell's equations, but as a physically motivated closure
for a specific class of states used as initial data in the simulations.

\subsection{Guided photon mode and effective-medium quantities}

We consider a straight waveguide oriented along the $x$-axis, with
transverse coordinates $(y,z)$ and spatial position
$\mathbf x = (x,y,z)$. In the effective electromagnetic description the
guided mode is represented by fields
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf E(\mathbf x,t), \qquad \mathbf B(\mathbf x,t),
\end{equation}
propagating in a homogeneous medium characterized by effective
parameters $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}$ and $\mu_{\mathrm{eff}}$.
Consistency with the emergent Maxwell wave equation
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:maxwell-wave}) and the static Poisson limit fixes the
usual relation
\begin{equation}
  c^2
  \;=\;
  \frac{1}{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}\mu_{\mathrm{eff}}}\,,
  \label{eq:app-c2}
\end{equation}
which identifies $c$ with the wave speed of small disturbances in the
medium, in agreement with Eq.~\eqref{eq:c-squared-relation}.

The local energy density and Poynting vector of the effective
electromagnetic field are
\begin{equation}
  u_{\mathrm{EM}}(\mathbf x,t)
  = \frac{1}{2}\Bigl(
      \varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}\,|\mathbf E(\mathbf x,t)|^2
      + \frac{|\mathbf B(\mathbf x,t)|^2}{\mu_{\mathrm{eff}}}
    \Bigr),
  \qquad
  \mathbf S(\mathbf x,t)
  = \frac{1}{\mu_{\mathrm{eff}}}\,\mathbf E(\mathbf x,t)\times\mathbf B(\mathbf x,t).
  \label{eq:app-u-S}
\end{equation}
For a monochromatic null wave with $|\mathbf E| = c|\mathbf B|$ one has
$u_{\mathrm{EM}} = \varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}|\mathbf E|^2
                 = |\mathbf B|^2/\mu_{\mathrm{eff}}$
and
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf S
  = u_{\mathrm{EM}}\,\mathbf v_{\mathrm{energy}},
  \qquad
  |\mathbf v_{\mathrm{energy}}| \approx c,
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf v_{\mathrm{energy}}$ is the usual energy-transport
velocity of the guided mode, aligned with the waveguide axis in the
straight case.

\subsection{Amplitude mode and local energy matching}
\label{app:inverse-amplitude}

On the microscopic side, the brane dynamics are governed by the
Lagrangian density~\eqref{eq:brane-lagrangian},
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}
  = \frac{\rho_m}{2}|\partial_t\mathbf X|^2
    - \frac{T}{2}\mathrm{tr}(E)
    - \mu |E|^2
    - \frac{\kappa}{2}|b|^2,
\end{equation}
where $\rho_m$ is the mass density and $T$ the effective tension of the
medium. In the static gauge used throughout the paper we write
\begin{equation}
  X^a(x,t) = x^a, \qquad X^4(x,t) = \xi_\perp(x,t),
\end{equation}
so that $\xi_\perp$ denotes the normal displacement (amplitude) of the
brane in the fourth embedding direction.

For small, predominantly longitudinal deformations along the waveguide
we can approximate the dynamics of $\xi_\perp$ by an effective
one-dimensional wave equation along the guide coordinate $s$,
\begin{equation}
  \rho_m\,\partial_{tt}\xi_\perp(s,t)
  \;=\;
  T\,\partial_{ss}\xi_\perp(s,t),
  \qquad
  c^2
  \;=\;
  \frac{T}{\rho_m},
  \label{eq:app-1d-wave}
\end{equation}
with local energy density
\begin{equation}
  u_{\mathrm{brane}}(s,t)
  = \frac{1}{2}\rho_m\bigl(\partial_t\xi_\perp\bigr)^2
    + \frac{1}{2}T\bigl(\partial_s\xi_\perp\bigr)^2.
  \label{eq:app-u-brane}
\end{equation}

For a monochromatic traveling wave at angular frequency $\omega$,
\begin{equation}
  \xi_\perp(s,t)
  = A(s)\,\cos\!\bigl(ks - \omega t + \phi(s)\bigr),
  \qquad
  \omega = c k,
  \label{eq:app-xi-traveling}
\end{equation}
one finds
\begin{equation}
  \partial_t\xi_\perp
    = \omega A(s)\,\sin(\dots),
  \qquad
  \partial_s\xi_\perp
    = -kA(s)\,\sin(\dots),
\end{equation}
and the corresponding energy density becomes
\begin{equation}
  u_{\mathrm{brane}}(s,t)
  =
  \frac{1}{2}\rho_m\omega^2 A^2(s)\sin^2(\dots)
  + \frac{1}{2}T k^2 A^2(s)\sin^2(\dots)
  =
  \rho_m\omega^2 A^2(s)\sin^2(\dots),
\end{equation}
where we used $T k^2 = \rho_m\omega^2$. Averaging over one temporal
period of the internal oscillation yields
\begin{equation}
  \bigl\langle u_{\mathrm{brane}}(s)\bigr\rangle_t
  = \frac{1}{2}\rho_m\omega^2 A^2(s).
  \label{eq:app-u-brane-avg}
\end{equation}

The inverse mapping for the amplitude is now obtained by demanding
\emph{local energy matching} between the effective electromagnetic
description and the microscopic brane mode: at each point on the
waveguide we impose
\begin{equation}
  \bigl\langle u_{\mathrm{brane}}(s)\bigr\rangle_t
  \;\approx\;
  \bigl\langle u_{\mathrm{EM}}(\mathbf x(s))\bigr\rangle_t.
\end{equation}
For a monochromatic guided mode the time-averaged electromagnetic
energy density is
\begin{equation}
  \bigl\langle u_{\mathrm{EM}}(\mathbf x)\bigr\rangle_t
  = \frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}\,E_0^2(\mathbf x)
  = \frac{1}{2}\frac{B_0^2(\mathbf x)}{\mu_{\mathrm{eff}}},
\end{equation}
with $E_0(\mathbf x)$ and $B_0(\mathbf x)$ the field amplitudes at
$\mathbf x$. Equating this with Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-u-brane-avg} gives the
amplitude magnitude
\begin{equation}
  A(\mathbf x)
  =
  \sqrt{\frac{2\,u_{\mathrm{EM}}(\mathbf x)}{\rho_m\,\omega^2}}
  \,.
  \label{eq:app-A-from-uEM}
\end{equation}
This relation is the core of the amplitude part of the inverse mapping:
it assigns a normal-displacement amplitude $A(\mathbf x)$ to each point
along the waveguide in such a way that the microscopic brane mode
carries the same local energy density as the effective electromagnetic
guided mode.

Given $A(\mathbf x)$ and the electromagnetic phase
$\phi_{\mathrm{EM}}(\mathbf x)$ of the guided mode, we can choose
initial conditions for $\xi_\perp$ at $t=0$ as
\begin{equation}
  \xi_\perp(\mathbf x,0)
  = A(\mathbf x)\,\cos\phi_{\mathrm{EM}}(\mathbf x),
  \qquad
  \partial_t\xi_\perp(\mathbf x,0)
  = -\,\omega\,A(\mathbf x)\,\sin\phi_{\mathrm{EM}}(\mathbf x).
  \label{eq:app-xi-initial}
\end{equation}
In numerical experiments we typically identify $\xi_\perp$ with the
fourth embedding component $X^4$ in the small-slope regime,
$\xi_\perp\approx X^4$, and restrict $A$ to remain within the linear
elastic range, so that higher-order geometric nonlinearities are
controlled by the subsequent evolution rather than the initial
conditions.

\subsection{Poynting vector and lateral velocities}
\label{app:poynting-lateral}

In the ontology of this model, magnetic effects are not independent
degrees of freedom but arise as \emph{relativistic manifestations} of
electric fields in motion. At the level of the effective electromagnetic
description this is encoded in the field tensor $F_{\mu\nu}$ and the
Lorentz transformation properties of $(\mathbf E,\mathbf B)$. At the
microscopic level we instead interpret the energy flow associated with
$\mathbf B$ as a statement about the \emph{lateral motion} of brane
material along the waveguide.

For any electromagnetic configuration described by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-u-S} we can define an energy-transport velocity
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf v_{\mathrm{energy}}(\mathbf x,t)
  \;:=\;
  \frac{\mathbf S(\mathbf x,t)}{u_{\mathrm{EM}}(\mathbf x,t)}.
  \label{eq:app-v-energy}
\end{equation}
For a null guided mode this satisfies
$|\mathbf v_{\mathrm{energy}}|\approx c$ and is directed along the
waveguide axis. We identify this velocity with the tangential velocity
of brane elements in the embedding space. In the static gauge where the
spatial embedding coordinates are
$\mathbf X_\parallel(x,t) = (X^1,X^2,X^3)$, the initial lateral
velocities at $t=0$ are therefore chosen as
\begin{equation}
  \partial_t\mathbf X_\parallel(\mathbf x,0)
  =
  \mathbf v_{\mathrm{lat}}(\mathbf x)
  :=
  \mathbf v_{\mathrm{energy}}(\mathbf x,0)
  =
  \frac{\mathbf S(\mathbf x,0)}{u_{\mathrm{EM}}(\mathbf x,0)}.
  \label{eq:app-Xparallel-initial}
\end{equation}
The normal velocity $\partial_t X^4(\mathbf x,0)$ is already fixed by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-xi-initial}. In regions where $u_{\mathrm{EM}}$ is
negligible, the ratio in Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-Xparallel-initial} is
suppressed and the brane remains approximately at rest in the tangential
directions, as expected for vacuum regions outside the guided mode.

In a Lorentz-covariant language one may view this prescription as
selecting, at each point along the waveguide, a local frame in which the
field is predominantly electric and relating the observed magnetic
component to the lateral motion of the underlying amplitude deformation.
Here we bypass the explicit frame transformation and use the Poynting
vector directly as a proxy for the microscopic energy flow.

\subsection{Extension to the toroidal double loop and limitations}

The construction above is local in $\mathbf x$ and does not rely on the
global geometry of the waveguide. It therefore carries over to the
toroidal electron picture of Appendix~\ref{app:tubular-electron} without
modification: one replaces the straight waveguide coordinate $s$ by the
longitudinal coordinate $z$ along the centerline $\mathbf C(z)$ of the
double loop, uses the tubular embedding
$\mathbf r(z,x,y)$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:tubular-embedding}, and applies
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:app-A-from-uEM}--\eqref{eq:app-Xparallel-initial}
pointwise along the strip. The only additional requirement is that the
local curvature of the strip remain small compared to the wavelength of
the guided mode, so that the local plane-wave approximation leading to
Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-1d-wave} remains valid.

It is important to emphasize the limited scope of this inverse mapping:

\begin{itemize}
  \item It applies to single, monochromatic guided modes in an effective
    medium characterized by $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}$ and
    $\mu_{\mathrm{eff}}$ obeying Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-c2}. It is not
    intended as a general inversion procedure for arbitrary
    electromagnetic field configurations or superpositions.

  \item The parameters $\rho_m$, $T$ and $\omega$ entering
    Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-A-from-uEM} are those of the microscopic brane
    model, not free continuum fit parameters. In practice they are fixed
    by the Compton-scale calibration and the elastic constants entering
    Eq.~\eqref{eq:brane-lagrangian}.

  \item The mapping is used as a \emph{closure prescription} for
    constructing initial brane configurations in numerical experiments.
    The emergent electromagnetic fields at later times are still
    obtained by the \emph{forward} brane $\to$ EM dictionary of the main
    text, via $\bar X^4$, the Poisson relation and the Maxwell-type wave
    equation~\eqref{eq:maxwell-wave}.
\end{itemize}

Within these limitations, the inverse mapping of this appendix provides
a concrete and internally consistent way to embed classical guided
photon modes into the microscopic brane dynamics, allowing direct
comparisons between simulations and the familiar Maxwellian description.
