## 🔸 1. Stability Through Nonlinearity

In many physical systems, **solitons** arise due to a **balance** between:

* **Dispersion**: which tends to spread out the wave packet,
* **Nonlinearity**: which tends to focus or contract it.

In systems like the **nonlinear Schrödinger equation**, **Korteweg-de Vries equation**, or even **Yang–Mills fields**, solitons emerge as stable, localized entities.

➡️ **In your model**, this would mean the electron is a **nonlinear excitation** of the field, where:

* Dispersion from Fourier spread is canceled by a threshold nonlinearity (like ReLU or Heaviside),
* This prevents the wave packet from diffusing.

Thus, **nonlinearity "locks" the structure**, preventing decay or dispersion.

## 🔸 2. Quantization Through Resonance Conditions

A stable soliton often requires **boundary conditions** or **resonance constraints** to exist. In a wave-based universe, this could mean:

* Only **certain Fourier configurations** are dynamically stable,
* These configurations correspond to **quantized** values of energy, momentum, spin, charge, etc.

➡️ This could explain:

* **Why electrons have a fixed mass and charge**: their soliton mode only exists for certain field amplitudes and configurations,
* **Why no half-electrons** are seen: because the field doesn't support "partial" soliton solutions.

This mirrors how allowed standing waves in a cavity form only at certain frequencies.

* The structure of the soliton is like a "knot" in the field,

Examples:

* **Skyrmions** (in nuclear and condensed matter physics),
* **Topological defects** like vortices or domain walls,
* **Hopfions**, where the field wraps around itself in higher dimensions.

➡️ In this view, the **electron’s identity** could arise from:
