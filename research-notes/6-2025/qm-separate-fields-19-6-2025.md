This is a profound and foundational question in quantum field theory (QFT), and it gets right to the heart of whether fields in QFT are **ontological entities** (things that really exist) or **epistemic tools** (mathematical constructs to describe observations).

Let’s unpack the issue:

---

### 💡 The Puzzle

You are observing a tension between:

* **Fields with distinct wave behaviors**: For example, the electromagnetic field (massless, propagates at the speed of light, spin-1) vs. the electron field (massive, spin-½, described by the Dirac equation).
* **Their interactions**: Such as an electron emitting or absorbing a photon, which requires both fields to "talk to each other" in some meaningful, presumably physical way.
* **But** if these fields are "mere mathematical tools" or live in different abstract spaces, **how can they interact?**

---

### 🔬 How Interactions Are Defined in QFT

In QFT, **interactions are built into the Lagrangian** — the core object from which all dynamics are derived. For example:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{int}} = -e \bar{\psi} \gamma^\mu A_\mu \psi
$$

* $\psi$: Dirac spinor field (electron)
* $A_\mu$: Electromagnetic gauge field (photon)
* $e$: Coupling constant (electric charge)

Despite these fields being **mathematically very different objects**, they are multiplied together in the Lagrangian in a Lorentz-invariant way.

This works **because they are all defined over the same spacetime manifold**. So even though they "live" in different internal spaces (spinor, vector, scalar...), they **coexist at every spacetime point**, which allows the interactions to be defined **locally**.

---

### 🧠 Is It Just Math or Is It Physical?

1. **Instrumentalist view** (Copenhagen/QFT-as-calculation):

    * Fields are just computational devices.
    * Interactions mean: The combined Lagrangian gives correct predictions.
    * No deeper explanation is needed; it works.

2. **Realist view** (e.g. Quantum Field Realism):

    * Fields are real, each a kind of distributed entity in spacetime.
    * Interactions are real dynamical couplings in the underlying ontology.
    * But: Why there are distinct fields instead of one unified field is an open question.

3. **Geometric/Topological view**:

    * Fields may arise as **sections of fiber bundles**.
    * Interactions emerge from **how these bundles are glued together over spacetime**.
    * E.g., gauge interactions come from requiring local symmetry under a Lie group (leading to connection fields like $A_\mu$).

---

### 🔁 Why the Model Still Works Despite the Abstraction

Even if fields are just math:

* **They share the same coordinate space (spacetime)**.
* **They are constructed to respect symmetries**: Lorentz invariance, gauge invariance, etc.
* The mathematical structure of QFT **enforces constraints** that mimic real-world interaction patterns.
* The **path integral formulation** (e.g., summing over all field configurations) gives you transition amplitudes that match experimental results.

---

### 🤯 So How *Can* Separate Fields Interact?

Because:

* They are not entirely "separate": they are defined **on the same spacetime**, and their mathematical representations (e.g. spinor vs vector) can be **combined** into invariant quantities.
* Interactions are encoded in how these fields **are allowed to combine in the Lagrangian**, constrained by symmetry and dimensional analysis.
* Whether this is a description of a **real dynamical process** or a **bookkeeping trick** is interpretational.
