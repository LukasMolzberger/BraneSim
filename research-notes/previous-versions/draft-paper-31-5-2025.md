**Title:**
A Deterministic, Ontological Wave-Based Interpretation of Quantum Mechanics with Nonlinear Collapse and Emergent Gravitation

**Abstract:**
This paper presents a realist and deterministic interpretation of quantum mechanics that replaces the notion of fundamental particles with nonlinear threshold phenomena within a continuous wave medium. The model posits an ontologically real wavefunction that evolves according to a modified Schrödinger equation incorporating a local, nonlinear collapse mechanism. Quantum behavior, including apparent particle-like effects, is the result of threshold-triggered localization events within the medium. The theory maintains locality in its dynamics while allowing for global coherence in the wave structure, enabling it to reproduce quantum correlations without invoking nonlocal signaling or branching universes. Gravitation is interpreted as an emergent tension resulting from transverse wave contraction.

---

## 1. Introduction

Standard quantum mechanics treats the wavefunction as either a tool for calculating probabilities (epistemic) or as a real entity (ontic), but lacks a mechanism for how measurement outcomes emerge without invoking external observers or nonlocal effects. Many interpretations introduce ontologically extravagant elements (e.g., many-worlds) or sacrifice determinism and locality.

This paper proposes an alternative: a purely wave-based, deterministic interpretation in which all quantum behavior arises from local nonlinear dynamics within a continuous medium. No fundamental particles exist; instead, all quantization phenomena are emergent from interactions governed by a local threshold function, such as a Heaviside or ReLU-type nonlinearity.

---

## 2. Ontology of the Model

### 2a. Spin and Internal Degrees of Freedom

The model accommodates spin as an emergent property of the wavefunction, not as an intrinsic discrete label tied to point particles. Spinor-valued fields, such as those used in the Dirac or Pauli formulations, describe wavefunctions that transform nontrivially under spatial rotations.

In this interpretation, spin arises from the internal structure and symmetry of the wave field. During a threshold-induced localization event, the nonlinear mechanism causes the spinor components of the wavefunction to collapse into one of the allowed eigenmodes relative to the measurement axis — such as "up" or "down" in a Stern–Gerlach-type setup.

This process does not require quantized spin operators or second quantization; instead, the quantized spin outcomes are emergent from the wave's global structure and localized self-interference. Spin is treated analogously to angular momentum in wave systems such as optical vortices, where discrete behavior arises from boundary conditions and wave geometry.

The model thus remains compatible with observations of spin while maintaining its ontological commitment to wave-only fields and single-instance quantization via nonlinear thresholds.

* The wavefunction $\psi(\vec{r}, t)$ is ontologically real.
* No point particles exist fundamentally. What are interpreted as "particles" are localized energy transfers resulting from threshold-triggered nonlinear events in the medium.
* The wave medium underlies all physical processes, including what are called "virtual particles."
* The distinction between virtual and real particles (as used in Feynman diagrams) arises from whether the local wave intensity crosses the nonlinearity threshold.

## 3. Modified Schrödinger Equation

We propose the following form:

$i\hbar \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial t} = \left( -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \nabla^2 + V(\vec{r}) + \lambda \, \Theta(|\psi|^2 - \rho_c) \right) \psi$

Where:

* $\Theta$ is the Heaviside function or a smoothed approximation (e.g., sigmoid).
* $\lambda$ governs the nonlinearity strength.
* $\rho_c$ is the critical probability density threshold.

Above the threshold $\rho_c$, the nonlinear term induces a structural modification of the wavefunction that leads to localization. Importantly, the evolution remains energy-conserving.

## 4. Interpretation of Collapse

* Collapse is a dynamical, internal feature of the system, not tied to observation.
* No measurement postulate is needed.
* Collapse results in discrete energy transfer between fields, conserving total energy.
* Quantization arises not from imposed axioms but from the structure of threshold-triggered field interactions.

## 5. Emergent Gravitation

* The medium imposes lateral tension to allow wave propagation.
* Gravitational effects emerge as a result of this transverse contraction.
* There is no need for gravitons or curvature-based fundamental theories; gravitation is a secondary, emergent effect of wave dynamics in the medium.

## 6. Locality and Bell's Theorem

The model is deterministic and locally causal in its wave dynamics, but globally coherent:

* Each wave evolves based on local interactions.
* However, initial conditions and boundary constraints may enforce globally entangled structures.
* No superluminal signaling occurs; correlations arise from a shared global mode, not from causal influence across space.

This offers a route to escape the Bell theorem's constraints by denying statistical independence of measurement settings and embracing emergent non-factorizability in wave structure.

---

## 6a. Compatibility with Quantum Field Theory

A potential critique is that the model, being formulated in terms of a nonlinear Schrödinger equation, appears incompatible with standard Quantum Field Theory (QFT), which typically relies on linear operator algebras and second quantization involving creation and annihilation operators. However, this model fundamentally diverges from that structure: it does not assume second quantization.

Instead, quantization arises only once, through the deterministic, threshold-like nonlinearity in the field dynamics. There are no discrete particle states requiring operator-based transitions. All apparent 'quantized' phenomena — including energy transfer, localization, and measurement outcomes — emerge from wave interactions exceeding critical thresholds. This avoids the need for an explicit Fock space or particle ontology.

Nevertheless, the model is intended to remain compatible with the empirical framework of QFT. It can reproduce quantum field behavior in appropriate limits and may be recast in a field-theoretic language by replacing the Schrödinger equation with relativistic field equations incorporating nonlinear thresholds. This allows the model to integrate with QFT conceptually and potentially extend it with a deterministic foundation.

## 7. Comparison with GRW Theory

While this model shares some conceptual ground with the Ghirardi–Rimini–Weber (GRW) theory, it diverges in key assumptions and mechanisms:

| Aspect                    | GRW (Ghirardi–Rimini–Weber)          | This Model                                                |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Wavefunction Ontology** | Ontic — real physical state          | Ontic — real physical wave                                |
| **Collapse Mechanism**    | Stochastic, spontaneous localization | Deterministic, threshold-induced from wave interference   |
| **Trigger for Collapse**  | Fundamental random jumps             | Emergent threshold crossing via constructive wave overlap |
| **Particles**             | Real post-collapse entities          | Illusory — all effects arise from localized waves         |
| **Superdeterminism**      | No                                   | Yes — all initial conditions fully determine outcomes     |
| **Quantization**          | Imposed via stochastic rules         | Emergent from nonlinear wave dynamics                     |
| **Locality**              | Nonlocal collapse                    | Local dynamics, global coherence, no signaling            |
| **Gravitation**           | Not addressed                        | Emergent from wave tension                                |

This model retains GRW’s realism but replaces indeterminism with structured determinism and avoids introducing new constants or postulates beyond the medium's nonlinear response.

## 8. Predictions and Experimental Relevance

The model leads to testable deviations from standard quantum mechanics:

* Disappearance of interference at high local intensities (due to collapse trigger).
* Mass- and complexity-dependent quantum-to-classical transition.
* Delayed localization in weak-field interactions.
* Potential gravitational anomalies at quantum scale due to lateral wave tension.

---

## 9. Conclusion

This interpretation replaces the problematic dualism of wave and particle with a purely wave-based ontology governed by local nonlinear dynamics. It preserves determinism, rejects metaphysical excesses like many-worlds, and interprets gravitation as a natural consequence of wave propagation in a medium. It stands as a conceptually coherent and experimentally accessible alternative to both standard quantum mechanics and popular realist interpretations.
