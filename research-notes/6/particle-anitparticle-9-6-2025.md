You're absolutely right in pointing out a major puzzle in physics: the **matter-antimatter asymmetry**.

### Background:

Quantum field theory (QFT) predicts that every **fermion field** (like the electron field or proton field) has corresponding **antiparticles** (positron, antiproton). When particles are created from energy (e.g., via pair production), the theory is **CPT symmetric**, meaning it should always create equal numbers of particles and antiparticles.

### Observation:

But the **observable universe** contains almost exclusively **matter**, not antimatter. This leads to the key question:

> Why is there far more matter than antimatter in the universe?

---

### Classical (Standard Model-based) Explanation Attempt:

The most accepted classical explanation is **baryogenesis**, which is the hypothetical process that generated this asymmetry in the early universe. It relies on a set of three criteria known as the **Sakharov conditions**, proposed by **Andrei Sakharov** in 1967:

---

### ✅ **Sakharov Conditions** (necessary for matter-antimatter asymmetry):

1. **Baryon number violation**
   There must be reactions that do not conserve the number of baryons (like protons and neutrons).

2. **C-symmetry and CP-symmetry violation**

    * **C-symmetry (Charge Conjugation)**: transforms particles into antiparticles.
    * **CP-symmetry**: applies both C and P (Parity – spatial inversion).
      If CP is violated, then reactions can favor matter over antimatter.

3. **Departure from thermal equilibrium**
   In a perfectly thermalized state, symmetry is maintained. Out-of-equilibrium conditions (e.g., during cosmic inflation or early phase transitions) allow asymmetries to "freeze in."

---

### Status in the Standard Model:

* The Standard Model does allow for **CP violation** (e.g., in the weak interaction via the CKM matrix),
  but **not nearly enough** to explain the observed asymmetry (only accounts for a **tiny fraction** of the matter dominance).
* **Baryon number violation** is not allowed in standard QFT, but may occur via **nonperturbative processes** (like sphalerons) at high energies in the early universe.
* **Grand Unified Theories (GUTs)** and **leptogenesis** are beyond-the-Standard-Model extensions that try to account for these issues.

---

### Summary:

> **The classical explanation involves baryogenesis under the Sakharov conditions, but the Standard Model alone cannot account for the observed matter-antimatter imbalance. This strongly suggests new physics beyond the Standard Model.**
