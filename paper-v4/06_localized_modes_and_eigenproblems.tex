\section{Localized modes and self-adjoint eigenproblems}
\label{sec:localized}

The central physical question for the substrate proposal is whether the same microscopic dynamics can support robust localized states.
If such states do not exist, the framework reduces to an unusual wave medium.
If they do exist, then particle-like behavior can be discussed without introducing fundamental point objects.
This section formulates the localized-mode problem in the most economical way.

\subsection{Linearization about symmetric backgrounds and self-adjointness}

Let $\vecX_0(x)$ be a time-independent background embedding representing a localized deformation region.
Write
\begin{equation}
  \vecX(x,t)=\vecX_0(x)+\bm{\eta}(x,t)
\end{equation}
with $\bm{\eta}$ small.
Because the equations of motion derive from the action \eqref{eq:action}, the linearized operator is obtained from the second variation of that action and is therefore self-adjoint with respect to the kinetic-energy inner product
\begin{equation}
  \langle \bm{\eta},\bm{\zeta}\rangle
  :=\int_{\Omega} \rho_m\,\bm{\eta}(x)\cdot \bm{\zeta}(x)\,\dd^3x,
  \label{eq:inner-product}
\end{equation}
subject to the chosen boundary conditions.
Thus orthogonality and real mode frequencies are not additional assumptions; they are consequences of variational structure.

\subsection{Angular structure, spherical families, and cubic corrections}

For a spherically symmetric background, the linearized operator commutes with the $SO(3)$ action on the intrinsic coordinates, so the angular dependence may be expanded in spherical harmonics $Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\varphi)$.
This gives the natural starting point for classifying localized modes.
The actual microscopic lattice is cubic rather than rotationally invariant, so exact degeneracies will generally be split at sufficiently short wavelengths.
Nevertheless, the spherical classification remains the correct long-wavelength organizing principle for localized states whose core size is large compared to the lattice spacing.

\subsection{Energy normalization and physical targets}

The linearized eigenproblem fixes spatial structure and oscillation frequencies but does not fix overall amplitude.
Physical amplitude must be selected by the nonlinear theory together with an energy target.
Accordingly, the relevant normalization condition is
\begin{equation}
  \overline{E}[\vecX] = M_{\mathrm{target}} c^2,
  \label{eq:rest-energy-normalization}
\end{equation}
where $M_{\mathrm{target}}$ is the physical mass assigned to the localized mode under study.
For the present program, proton- and neutron-scale targets are the most natural first cases because they match the intrinsic three-channel structure of the cubic carrier sector more directly than an electron-like state does.

\subsection{Confinement as nonlinear self-guidance}

A localized mode requires a confinement mechanism.
In a purely linear system, packets generically disperse or radiate away.
A useful conceptual benchmark is the toroidal-electron literature, which correctly emphasizes that ordinary weak gravitational curvature is far too small to confine a Compton-scale wave mode \citep{Williamson1997,vanderMark2000LightHeavy}.
The present framework adopts that confinement requirement but assigns the trapping mechanism to the substrate's own constitutive and geometric nonlinearity.
Large-amplitude deformation changes the local stress response and therefore the local propagation operator for nearby fluctuations.
A sufficiently structured excitation can then ``write'' an effective medium for itself and remain self-guided.

\subsection{Three-channel localized modes as baryon-like targets}

On the cubic lattice the most informative localized states are those that significantly occupy the axis-aligned triplet sector.
Such states need not be identified immediately with literal quarks.
The narrower and more testable statement is that a stable three-channel bound configuration would be the natural substrate analogue of a baryonic object.
A proton-like and a neutron-like state would then correspond to two nearby stable solutions with similar geometric scale and total energy but different far-field holonomy or charge-like diagnostics.
This is the point at which the localized-mode program meets the geometric-phase program:
one must solve for the nonlinear core and the long-range connection data at the same time.

\subsection{Connection to Berry/Wilczek--Zee holonomy and spin}

Localized modes can carry internal polarization structure, and the Berry/Wilczek--Zee connections of \Cref{sec:geophase} can be evaluated either on loops surrounding a localized core or along adiabatic deformations of a localized family.
In this way, long-range ``charge''-like labels are interpreted as holonomy data of the surrounding carrier sector, while the spin-$\tfrac12$ $4\pi$ aspect is interpreted as spinorial transport of an internal two-level subspace.
The crucial open task is to demonstrate this numerically for actual localized solutions of the substrate equations.
