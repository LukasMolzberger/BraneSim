\begin{abstract}
We propose a deterministic substrate theory in which observable physics arises from waves on a
three-dimensional elastic medium (a ``3D brane'') embedded in an ambient four-dimensional Euclidean space,
with an external time parameter.
The continuum model is formulated as an isotropic hyperelastic action in intrinsic brane coordinates,
so rotational invariance within the brane is built in at the continuum level.

The central additional postulate is the existence of an ordered, narrowband carrier sector
(assumed to live primarily in a longitudinal polarization branch) that provides a coherent
phase reference.
We show how adiabatic transport of a polarized multi-component wave state on this substrate induces
a Berry connection (rank-1) or, for near-degenerate subspaces, a Wilczek--Zee connection (non-Abelian).
These geometric connections are interpreted as the natural origin of gauge degrees of freedom in a
coarse-grained description, and are connected to a Dirac-envelope formulation in which
``particle'' structure (solitonic topology/geometry and its internal labels) is conceptually separated
from the underlying local phase field, even though the standard Dirac equation mixes both.

In this framework, the probabilistic interpretation of quantum theory is not taken as fundamental:
apparent point-like interactions are attributed to Fourier-localization constraints and
threshold-like nonlinear exchange events in a deterministic wave medium.
Particle-like states are localized solitons whose spatial structure is organized by spherical
symmetry and spherical-harmonic mode families; the spin-$\tfrac12$ $4\pi$ aspect is attributed to
geometric phase/holonomy rather than to literal multi-winding of a photon trajectory.

Discrete numerical realizations are treated only as an implementation reference and placed in an appendix.
\end{abstract}
