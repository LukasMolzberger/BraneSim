\section{The retrocausal worldtube interpretation}
\label{sec:worldtube}

\subsection{Time-symmetry of the substrate action}
\label{subsec:time-symmetry}

The brane action of Paper~I (\Cref{eq:action}) is time-symmetric.
Its Lorentzian sign structure selects a \emph{kind} of direction --- timelike vs spacelike ---
but the action is invariant under reversal of the timelike coordinate, $t\mapsto -t$.
Neither the kinetic term nor the elastic potential distinguishes past from future.
This means the substrate has no intrinsic arrow of time: both forward-in-time and
backward-in-time solutions to \Cref{eq:eom} of Paper~I are equally valid.

\subsection{The arrow of time: thermodynamic, with chirality fixing matter vs antimatter}
\label{subsec:soliton-chirality}

If the substrate has no intrinsic arrow of time, where does the empirical arrow come from?
Two distinct questions hide here, and the substrate answers them separately --- so the arrow
should not be attributed to soliton chirality alone.

\paragraph{The experienced arrow is thermodynamic.}
The arrow we actually experience --- irreversibility, records of the past but not the future, the
felt direction of causation --- is not produced by the substrate dynamics, which are
time-symmetric (\Cref{subsec:time-symmetry}), and it is not produced by soliton chirality either.
It is the \emph{thermodynamic} arrow: it rests on a low-entropy boundary condition at one temporal
end of the brane --- the cosmological ``past hypothesis''
\citep{Albert2000TimeAndChance,PriceWharton2015Retrocausality}, the same low-entropy datum named
as the one residual tuning of the ontology (\Cref{subsec:no-superdeterminism}).
The entropy gradient it establishes is what orients memory, measurement records, and the
psychological sense of pastness.
On this point the present reading simply inherits the standard time-symmetric account, in which
the arrow of experienced causality is statistical-mechanical, not dynamical.

\paragraph{Chirality fixes matter versus antimatter.}
Soliton chirality answers a \emph{different} question: given the brane's timelike axis, a
localized mode (Paper~IV \Cref{sec:matter}) may propagate forward or backward along it --- a
topological/dynamical property of the solution analogous to the handedness of a Skyrme texture.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Matter solitons} are forward-propagating worldtubes: their chirality orients the
  worldtube toward the future.
  \item \textbf{Antimatter solitons} are backward-propagating worldtubes: their chirality orients
  the worldtube toward the past.
\end{itemize}
This realizes the Feynman--Stueckelberg interpretation of antiparticles as time-reversed particles
\citep{Stueckelberg1941,Feynman1949ApproachQED} at the level of substrate soliton solutions,
rather than as an interpretive remark attached to a Lorentz-invariant field theory after the fact.
Chirality therefore fixes the particle/antiparticle distinction and the local time-orientation of
a given worldtube; it does \emph{not} by itself supply the experienced arrow, which is the
entropic one above.

\paragraph{Cosmological asymmetry.}
The empirical excess of matter over antimatter is, on this reading, a \emph{geometric
initial-condition asymmetry}: matter and antimatter solitons emerge from the Big Bang vertex
propagating in opposite time directions along the brane's selected timelike axis, and our
observable universe is the forward-propagating branch.
This differs structurally from Sakharov-condition baryogenesis \citep{Sakharov1967CPviolation},
which requires the same time direction to host both matter and antimatter with a small dynamical
asymmetry favouring matter.
Here, the asymmetry is not dynamical but geometric: the two branches simply do not share the same
observable volume.

\subsection{Entanglement as V-branching worldtubes}
\label{subsec:v-branching}

An entangled pair, on the present reading, is \emph{one} branching 4D world-volume whose worldtube
splits at a V-vertex in the shared past.
The branches carry the entangled excitations themselves; for the polarization-entangled photon
pair we commit to (\Cref{subsec:photon-v-worldtube}) these are unconfined carrier waves --- the
photons --- and the only solitons involved are the bound electrons in the detectors, not the
entangled objects.

\paragraph{Mechanism.}
Each branch of the world-volume propagates forward to a measurement event.
The boundary condition imposed by a measurement on one branch is not transmitted
\emph{sideways} through space to the other branch (which would be a spacelike influence,
violating (L)).
Instead, it propagates \emph{backward} along the branch's own worldtube to the V-vertex in the
shared past, and from there \emph{forward} along the other branch to the distant measurement.
The V-vertex is the mediator; no spacelike signal is required.

\paragraph{Locality along each worldline.}
The correlation is:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{local along each worldline}: no influence crosses a spacelike boundary at any
  step of the propagation.
  \item \emph{deterministic given the full 4D configuration}: the correlation is fixed by the
  worldtube geometry and the V-vertex boundary condition.
  \item \emph{Bell-inequality-violating}: the joint measurement context --- both settings $a$ and
  $b$ --- affects the past of both branches via the V-vertex, so the standard factorizability
  assumption of Bell's derivation fails.
\end{itemize}
The 4D world-volume picture promotes ``spooky action at a distance'' to ``ordinary continuity of
one extended elastic object.''

\paragraph{No preferred foliation required.}
Unlike Bohmian mechanics, this picture does not require a preferred spacelike foliation.
The correlation propagates along the worldtube in a covariant, 4D-in-4D sense.
The effective Lorentz symmetry of Paper~II constrains which foliations are observationally
available to inside observers, but the worldtube itself is a 4D geometric object prior to any
foliation choice.

\subsection{The polarization-entangled photon pair, formalized}
\label{subsec:photon-v-worldtube}

The canonical laboratory realization of a V-branching worldtube is the polarization-entangled
photon pair of the entangled-photon Bell tests recognized by the 2022 Nobel Prize --- in
particular the strict-Einstein-locality experiment of Weihs et al.\ \citep{Weihs1998}, built on the
type-II down-conversion source of Kwiat et al.\ \citep{Kwiat1995}, and the loophole-free photon
test of the Vienna group \citep{Giustina2015}. We take that construct as the concrete target for
an eventual exact substrate solution and state its structure precisely.

\paragraph{Apparatus, in substrate terms.}
A pump excitation drives a nonlinear matter vertex --- the down-conversion crystal, a localized
region of bound matter solitons --- in which one carrier wavepacket is converted into two. The two
daughters are unconfined carrier waves, not solitons (Paper~I \Cref{subsec:continuous-waves},
Paper~III \Cref{subsec:spinorial-holonomy}; \Cref{subsec:non-probabilistic}). The entangled pair
is therefore a single branching \emph{carrier} world-volume --- one tube that splits at the source
vertex $S$ into two arms, $A$ (toward Alice) and $B$ (toward Bob). The V-vertex of
\Cref{subsec:v-branching} is here the source event $S$; the two measurement endpoints are detector
events $D_A$, $D_B$ at which each arm couples to a bound electron soliton.

\paragraph{State and boundary data.}
Along each arm the carrier is described by the slowly varying ansatz of Paper~III
\eqref{eq:quasi-monochromatic}, with a normalized transverse polarization
$\bm{\epsilon}_A(x),\bm{\epsilon}_B(x)\in\C^{2}$. The source vertex imposes a \emph{joint} boundary
condition relating the two polarizations --- the rotationally invariant singlet
\begin{equation}
  \lvert\Psi^-\rangle \;=\; \tfrac{1}{\sqrt 2}\bigl(\lvert H\rangle_A\lvert V\rangle_B
    - \lvert V\rangle_A\lvert H\rangle_B\bigr),
  \label{eq:singlet}
\end{equation}
whose substrate counterpart is the requirement that the two arms leave $S$ with anticorrelated
transverse polarization frames, invariantly under a common rotation of both frames (the defining
property of the singlet). Each detector imposes a \emph{local} boundary condition: an analyzer at
orientation $a$ (Alice) or $b$ (Bob) selects a polarization axis, and the bound electron
transition renders the outcome one of two discrete values $A_a, B_b\in\{+1,-1\}$ (the discreteness
supplied by the detector, \Cref{subsec:non-probabilistic}).

\paragraph{The correlation to be reproduced.}
For the singlet \eqref{eq:singlet} measured at linear-analyzer angles $a$ and $b$, quantum
mechanics --- and the experiments \citep{Weihs1998,Giustina2015} --- give the joint correlation
\begin{equation}
  E(a,b) \;=\; \langle A_a\, B_b\rangle \;=\; -\cos\!\bigl(2(a-b)\bigr),
  \label{eq:cos-correlation}
\end{equation}
which violates the CHSH bound: with $(a,a',b,b')=(0,\tfrac{\pi}{4},\tfrac{\pi}{8},\tfrac{3\pi}{8})$,
\begin{equation}
  S \;=\; E(a,b)-E(a,b')+E(a',b)+E(a',b') \;=\; 2\sqrt 2 \;>\; 2 .
  \label{eq:chsh-tsirelson}
\end{equation}
Equation \eqref{eq:cos-correlation} is the exact target: a successful substrate derivation must
produce it from the branching-carrier world-volume with the source and detector boundary data
above, and with \emph{no} spacelike coupling between the arms.

\paragraph{How the worldtube is meant to supply the correlation.}
The mechanism of \Cref{subsec:v-branching} applies verbatim with $S$ as the V-vertex. The analyzer
setting $a$ enters only as a \emph{future} boundary condition on arm $A$ at $D_A$; because the
substrate action is time-symmetric (\Cref{subsec:time-symmetry}), that condition propagates
backward along $A$ to $S$ and forward along $B$ to $D_B$, so the joint statistics can depend on
both $a$ and $b$ while every influence remains timelike-along-a-worldline. The factor that makes
this Bell-relevant rather than a classical common cause is that the setting-dependent boundary
data are imposed \emph{after} emission: the $-\cos 2(a-b)$ law of \eqref{eq:cos-correlation} is
required to emerge from the way the singlet condition at $S$ composes with the two analyzer
projections, which a setting-independent past state at $S$ cannot reproduce
(\Cref{subsec:no-superdeterminism}). Making \eqref{eq:cos-correlation} follow quantitatively from
this composition is the decisive open derivation, stated below.

\subsection{Implications for the simulation program}

The retrocausal worldtube reading reshapes how the simulation program of Papers~II--IV should
interpret its own results.

\paragraph{Eigenstates as worldtubes.}
A converged periodic-BC eigenstate (Paper~IV \Cref{subsec:periodic-bc}) is not a static snapshot;
it is a closed 4D worldtube on which the substrate field satisfies the time-symmetric equations of
motion with a periodic closure condition.
The carrier frequency $\omega_0$ fixed by the closure condition $\omega T = 2\pi n$ is the
timelike winding number of the worldtube, not a clock reading.

\paragraph{Entanglement test.}
The V-branching picture makes a concrete simulation prediction: two carrier wavepackets --- the
entangled-photon pair of \Cref{subsec:photon-v-worldtube} --- emitted with correlated
polarizations from a common source vertex should maintain those correlations over spacelike
separation in a way that, when the full 4D worldtube is inspected, is traceable to continuity
through the V-vertex, not to any spacelike link.
A simulation that finds correlation without spacelike signal is consistent with the retrocausal
reading; one that requires adding a nonlocal coupling is not.

\paragraph{Matter vs antimatter.}
Two soliton solutions with opposite chirality (forward vs backward propagating) should appear as
particle--antiparticle pairs: identical mass and spin, opposite quantum numbers, and opposite
time orientation of the closed worldtube.
A simulation that finds such paired solutions would directly confirm the soliton-chirality
mechanism.

\subsection{Open problems}
\label{subsec:bell-open}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Explicit chirality classification.}
  Define the soliton chirality quantum number precisely in terms of the topological winding
  (Paper~IV \Cref{eq:soliton-labels}) and the time orientation of the worldtube closure condition.
  Show that two solutions related by $t\mapsto -t$ carry opposite chirality.

  \item \textbf{V-vertex simulation.}
  Simulate the entangled-photon pair (\Cref{subsec:photon-v-worldtube}) as two carrier arms
  emitted from a common source vertex; verify that the subsequent 4D world-volumes share that
  V-vertex and exhibit correlated behavior at their respective detector endpoints without any
  spacelike signal.

  \item \textbf{Born-rule recovery.}
  Identify the substrate mechanism that produces apparent Born-rule statistics from the
  deterministic worldtube dynamics.
  This is the sole point at which probability enters the theory, and it enters as an emergent,
  derived account of ensembles rather than a fundamental law (\Cref{subsec:non-probabilistic}).
  The candidate mechanism (Paper~IV \Cref{subsec:mass-picture}): Fourier-localization constraints
  on wavepacket overlaps combined with nonlinear exchange events at the V-vertex.
  A quantitative model is not yet available.

  \item \textbf{Exact entangled-photon correlation (decisive target).}
  For the polarization-entangled photon pair of \Cref{subsec:photon-v-worldtube}, derive the
  singlet correlation $E(a,b)=-\cos 2(a-b)$ of \eqref{eq:cos-correlation} --- and hence the
  Tsirelson value $S=2\sqrt 2$ of \eqref{eq:chsh-tsirelson} --- directly from a branching-carrier
  world-volume: a source vertex $S$ enforcing the anticorrelated-frame (singlet) boundary
  condition \eqref{eq:singlet}, two arms carrying spin-$1$ polarization eigenbundles, and two
  detector boundary conditions at analyzer angles $a,b$ imposed at $D_A,D_B$.
  The derivation must reproduce \eqref{eq:cos-correlation} with all coupling timelike-along-an-arm
  (no spacelike link), and must show that the setting-dependent future boundary data --- not a
  setting-independent state at $S$ --- are what generate the $\cos 2(a-b)$ angular law
  (\Cref{subsec:no-superdeterminism}).
  This is the most direct quantitative falsifier of the retrocausal worldtube reading.
\end{enumerate}
