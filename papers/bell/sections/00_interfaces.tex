\section{The substrate, in brief}
\label{sec:substrate}

This paper is self-contained: the substrate model it rests on is stated here, with the full
construction and derivations in companion papers
\citep{MolzbergerSubstrate,MolzbergerGaugeColor}. Only the elements used below are included.

\paragraph{Ontology.}
The substrate is a four-dimensional cubic lattice of nodes --- a 4D \emph{brane} --- embedded with
codimension zero in a 4D Euclidean ambient space. Each node carries a displacement
$\vecX\in\R^{4}$, and nearest neighbours are coupled by central-force (spring) interactions. The
lattice is the entire physical content: there is no configuration space and no field beyond the
single real displacement field on the 4D world-volume.

\paragraph{Action, determinism, and locality.}
The dynamics follow from a local action of $T-V$ (kinetic minus elastic) form,
\begin{equation}
  S[\vecX]=\int \Lag\,\dd^4x ,\qquad
  \Lag=\text{(timelike-link terms)}-\text{(spacelike-link terms)} ,
  \label{eq:brane-action}
\end{equation}
whose Lorentzian sign structure --- opposite signs for timelike- and spacelike-direction link
terms --- singles out one of the four lattice directions as \emph{time}. The Euler--Lagrange
equations of motion
\begin{equation}
  \tfrac{\dd}{\dd t}\,\partial_{\dot{\vecX}}\Lag=\partial_{\vecX}\Lag
  \label{eq:brane-eom}
\end{equation}
are second-order and \emph{deterministic} (a complete configuration fixes all past and future
configurations) and \emph{local}: forces act only between spring-neighbours and propagate at
finite speed, set by the transverse and longitudinal wave speeds $c_T,c_L$. The substrate has no
superluminal channel.

\paragraph{Time-symmetry.}
The action \eqref{eq:brane-action} is invariant under reversal of the timelike coordinate
$t\mapsto -t$: it selects a \emph{kind} of direction (timelike vs spacelike) but not a
\emph{direction} along the timelike axis. The substrate therefore has no intrinsic arrow of time;
both forward- and backward-propagating solutions of \eqref{eq:brane-eom} are admissible, and a
configuration is fixed by boundary data on the past \emph{and} future endcaps of the 4D
world-volume.

\paragraph{Linear waves and the carrier.}
Small displacements are continuous waves, with a spectrum continuous up to the lattice-spacing
cutoff; nothing in the linear sector takes discrete values. The transverse polarization of a
narrowband wave propagating along an axis spans a two-dimensional space $\C^{2}$ --- the qubit
Hilbert space used below --- and is a spin-$1$ (vector) mode. Its slowly varying complex amplitude
\begin{equation}
  \psi=\xi+\tfrac{\ii}{\omega_0}\dot\xi \;=\; q+\ii p
  \label{eq:carrier}
\end{equation}
takes its imaginary unit from carrier rotation along the timelike axis, with $(q,p)$ the two field
quadratures. A free electromagnetic excitation is exactly such an unconfined linear carrier and is
not quantized at the substrate level.

\paragraph{Geometric nonlinearity.}
The spring energy depends on the geometric link length $|\Delta\vecX|$ (a norm), whose expansion
beyond quadratic order is the substrate's only anharmonicity, with strength fixed by the prestress
$\alpha$ and stiffness $k_s$:
\begin{equation}
  V\supset -\,k_s\,\alpha\,a\,|\Delta\vecX|
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \text{cubic/quartic couplings}\ \propto\ k_s\alpha/a .
  \label{eq:geom-nonlinear}
\end{equation}
This coupling binds confined modes and drives the source vertex of \Cref{sec:worldtube}.

\paragraph{Solitons, matter, and chirality.}
Confined, self-localized nonlinear solutions of \eqref{eq:brane-eom} are the matter particles;
their discrete labels (spin, charge, topological winding) are what \emph{quantization} means in
this framework --- a consequence of confinement geometry, not a substrate postulate. A soliton may
propagate forward or backward along the timelike axis: this \emph{chirality} distinguishes matter
(forward-propagating worldtubes) from antimatter (backward-propagating). The full soliton sector
is developed in a companion paper \citep{MolzbergerMatterMass}.
