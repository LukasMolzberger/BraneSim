Short answer: it’s a clever, visually appealing idea, but as it stands it’s not a quantitatively successful electron model. You could re-interpret it inside a 3-D brane in 4-D space, but you’d need extra (nonlinear/medium) physics and—crucially—new, testable calculations to meet modern constraints.

# What Williamson & van der Mark actually propose

* **Core claim (1997):** the electron is a single photon “twirled” into a **toroidal / double-loop** topology with circumference ~ one Compton wavelength. Spin, charge and magnetic moment are argued to emerge from the geometry and circulating Poynting flux. ([fondationlouisdebroglie.org][1])
* Follow-on writeups by Williamson reiterate the semi-classical, topological picture (electron as a localized photon/soliton). These are conceptual, not precision fits. ([quicycle.com][2])

# How it could map to “a 3-D brane in 4-D space”

* **Why a brane helps:** In vacuum, linear Maxwell equations don’t self-confine light. In a **medium** (your brane) or with **nonlinear electrodynamics**, stable knotted/toroidal electromagnetic structures (“Hopfions”) can exist, at least in principle/numerically. That’s the right *kind* of mechanism W&vdM would need but didn’t supply. ([Physical Review][3])
* **What you’d need to specify on the brane:**

    1. a confining term or dispersion that localizes EM energy into a torus,
    2. topological invariants (linking/Hopf charge) that correspond to electron quantum numbers,
    3. coupling rules to the 4-D bulk so standard QED interactions are recovered on the brane. (Hopfion reviews from EM/magnetism literature show the topology toolkit you’d borrow.) ([MDPI][4])

# Where the model runs into hard experimental walls

To be a *good* electron model, it must match these facts simultaneously:

1. **Pointlike scattering up to ~10⁻¹⁹ m.** LEP Bhabha scattering places **rₑ < 2.8×10⁻¹⁹ m (95% CL)**. Any finite torus radius must be small enough—and its form factor must look pointlike—across high-Q². ([arXiv][5])
2. **Magnetic moment (g-2).** The electron’s g agrees exquisitely with QED; any composite/toroidal model must reproduce the same number and radiative corrections (anomalous part). Current consensus notes the **electron** g matches SM very precisely (the muon is the puzzling one). ([arXiv][6])
3. **Tiny eEDM.** Latest measurements give **|dₑ| < 4.4×10⁻³⁰ e·cm** (and earlier ACME II was 1.1×10⁻²⁹ e·cm). Extended charge/magnetization textures often predict larger EDMs unless carefully engineered. ([jila.colorado.edu][7])

The 1997 W&vdM paper doesn’t derive a realistic electron form factor, g-2, or the EDM bound from its geometry; it provides a qualitative picture. That’s why it hasn’t displaced QED in mainstream use. ([fondationlouisdebroglie.org][1])

# Bottom line

* **Interesting as a topological/soliton *motif*** (especially if your brane provides confinement), **not** yet a viable replacement for the electron in QED. You’d need a full field theory on the brane that:
  • yields a stable toroidal solution with electron quantum numbers,
  • reproduces scattering as pointlike to 10⁻¹⁹ m,
  • matches **g-2** and the **EDM** limit, and
  • reduces to standard QED amplitudes for processes like Bhabha scattering. ([arXiv][5])

# If you want to pursue it inside your 3-D-brane program

Concrete next steps that could make or break the idea:

1. **Brane EM model:** Pick a minimal nonlinear/medium term (e.g., Born-Infeld-like or a dispersive constitutive law) on the brane and **numerically construct a toroidal Hopfion** at Compton scale; verify stability and quantized invariants. (Use the Hopfion literature as the construction playbook.) ([Physical Review][3])
2. **Quantum numbers from topology:** Prove how spin-½ and charge arise (e.g., double covering, Chern–Simons/Hopf index to charge mapping). This is the central theoretical gap in W&vdM. ([fondationlouisdebroglie.org][1])
3. **Observables:** Derive the **form factor** F(Q²) and **magnetic moment** (including radiative corrections) for your torus; check against LEP bounds and g-2. ([arXiv][5])
4. **Symmetry tests:** Show the construction naturally enforces a tiny EDM consistent with the 2023 limit. ([Science][8])
