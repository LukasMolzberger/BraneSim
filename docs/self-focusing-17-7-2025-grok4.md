### Formalization of Conditions and Constraints for Self-Focusing and Self-Trapping

Self-focusing and self-trapping are nonlinear phenomena where waves or beams concentrate energy spatially, counteracting natural dispersion (spreading due to diffraction or lattice effects). Self-focusing refers to the progressive narrowing or collapse of a wave packet, while self-trapping (or self-guiding) is the stable, non-dispersive propagation of a localized structure, often requiring a balance to prevent blow-up or decay.

These occur in systems governed by nonlinear wave equations, such as the Nonlinear Schrödinger Equation (NLS) or its variants, where nonlinearity arises from intensity-dependent terms (e.g., Kerr effect in optics) or geometric effects (e.g., in embedded media like your brane). #### General Conditions for Self-Focusing
Self-focusing initiates when nonlinearity dominates dispersion, leading to amplitude-dependent phase shifts or velocity changes that cause inward focusing.

1. **Nonlinearity Threshold**: The system must have a nonlinear term that increases the effective "refractive index" or wave speed with amplitude/intensity. For a wave ψ(x,t) in d dimensions, the generic NLS is:
   \[
   i \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial t} = -\frac{1}{2} \nabla^2 \psi + f(|\psi|^2) \psi,
   \]
   where f(|\psi|^2) is the nonlinear potential (e.g., f = -|\psi|^2 for focusing). Self-focusing occurs if the nonlinear coefficient γ > 0 (focusing type) and the initial amplitude A_0 satisfies γ A_0^2 > dispersion strength.

2. **Critical Power/Amplitude Criterion**: In optics or similar, self-focusing happens above a critical power P_crit. For a Gaussian beam in 2D:
   \[
   P_\text{crit} = \frac{\lambda^2}{8\pi n_0 n_2},

3. **Virial Theorem for Collapse**: From mathematical theory, self-focusing leads to singularity if the Hamiltonian H < 0 and the initial condition satisfies the virial identity:
   \[
   \frac{d^2}{dt^2} \int r^2 |\psi|^2 dr = 8H,

4. **Balance with Dispersion**: For initiation, the nonlinear length L_nl (where nonlinearity accumulates) must be shorter than the diffraction length L_diff:
