### 🧵 **Intuitive Picture (Tensioned String)**

On a tensioned string, waves typically obey the **wave equation**:

$$
\frac{\partial^2 y}{\partial t^2} = c^2 \frac{\partial^2 y}{\partial x^2}
$$

where $y(x, t)$ is the displacement at point $x$, and $c$ is the wave speed.

#### Now, let’s consider:

* A **low-frequency (LF)** wave: $y_L(x, t)$, slowly varying, long wavelength.
* A **high-frequency (HF)** wave: $y_H(x, t)$, rapidly oscillating, localized.

Assume:

$$
y(x, t) = y_L(x, t) + y_H(x, t)
$$

### 🧠 **Physical Interaction**

Although the wave equation is **linear**, in physical reality (especially on strings, membranes, or media with tension that varies with deformation), **nonlinear effects** or effective curvature can arise.

    * An **asymmetry** in tension distribution across the HF wave.
    * A **Doppler-like modulation** of the HF wave's phase or envelope.

2. **Group Velocity Shift**:
   If you treat the HF wave as a **wave packet**, then its motion depends on the **local group velocity**. A **tilted background** due to the LF wave can effectively change the propagation speed slightly, nudging the packet **to one side**.

3. **Geometrical Analogy (Analogue Gravity)**:

In curved spacetime analogies (e.g., in fluid dynamics or optical media), a **gradient in the background** acts like an effective force — bending, focusing, or displacing the HF wave.
